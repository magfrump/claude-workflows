## Verdict for C1: "US healthcare spending reached $4.3 trillion in 2023"

**Verdict:** Inaccurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** [observed]
**Scrutiny:** [abstract]

CMS's Office of the Actuary put 2023 US national health spending at **$4.9 trillion**, up 7.5% from 2022, in its Health Affairs study of December 18, 2024. The draft's figure is about $600 billion too low. CMS's 2022 total was $4.5 trillion, so $4.3 trillion is below even the year before. A mid-2024 AJMC article gave $4.8 trillion, but that was an earlier projection; the December 2024 historical estimate supersedes it. Confidence is Medium: there is one primary source (CMS/Health Affairs), seen only through search text that states the figure directly. A deep-read of the full CMS NHE tables would lift it to High.

**Sources:** Martin, Hartman et al., "National Health Expenditures In 2023: Faster Growth As Insurance Coverage And Utilization Increased," *Health Affairs*, Dec. 2024 (CMS Office of the Actuary); news coverage by Medical Economics, ASCP and AHA, Dec. 2024
**Citation:** "in 2023, health care spending in the United States reached $4.9 trillion and increased 7.5%" — [Health Affairs, 2024](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/full/10.1377/forefront.20241217.924408)

## Verdict for C2: "or 17.3% of GDP"

**Verdict:** Inaccurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** [observed]
**Scrutiny:** [abstract]

CMS reports health spending at **17.6% of GDP in 2023**, up from 17.4% in 2022. The draft's 17.3% matches CMS's *original* 2022 figure from its December 2023 release, which was later revised to 17.4%, so as a 2023 figure it is wrong. Confidence is Medium: one primary source (CMS/Health Affairs), seen only through search text.

**Sources:** CMS/Health Affairs NHE 2023 study (Dec. 2024); CMS "National Health Expenditures 2022 Highlights" fact sheet (Dec. 13, 2023)
**Citation:** "rising to 4.9 trillion dollars, equivalent to about 17.6 percent of gross domestic product" — [Health Affairs NHE 2023 press materials](https://www.healthaffairs.org/cms/10.1377/he20241230.971146/asset/nhe23-press-event-final.pdf)

## Verdict for C3: "the $3.8 trillion spent in 2019"

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** [observed]
**Scrutiny:** [abstract]

CMS's December 2020 release reported 2019 national health spending of about $3.8 trillion ($11,582 per person). CDC's *Health, United States* lists $3,795 billion for 2019, which agrees. That makes two independent primary sources, which would qualify for High, but both were seen only through search text, so the rating is capped at Medium. Opening the CMS historical tables would lift it to High.

**Sources:** CMS press release, "CMS Office of the Actuary Releases 2019 National Health Expenditures," Dec. 16, 2020; CDC/NCHS, *Health, United States, 2020–2021*, national health expenditures table
**Citation:** [CMS press release, Dec. 16, 2020](https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/press-releases/cms-office-actuary-releases-2019-national-health-expenditures); also the CDC table at https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/data/hus/2020-2021/HExpGDP.pdf

## Verdict for C4: "This represents a significant increase from the $3.8 trillion spent in 2019"

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** [inferred]
**Scrutiny:** [abstract]

The direction is right: spending did rise substantially between 2019 and 2023. How it was worked out: CMS reports $3.8T for 2019 (C3) and $4.9T for 2023 (C1), an increase of about $1.1T, or roughly 29%. With the draft's own $4.3T figure, the implied increase is only about $0.5T (about 13%), so the draft understates the size of the rise. As a share of GDP, CMS says 2023's 17.6% was "about the same" as 2019's (17.5% after revision), so the rise is in dollars, not in share of the economy. Confidence is Medium, resting on the same CMS sources as C1 and C3.

**Sources:** CMS/Health Affairs NHE 2023 study (Dec. 2024); CMS 2019 NHE release (Dec. 2020)
**Citation:** "about the same share as in 2019 (17.5 percent)" — CMS/Health Affairs NHE 2023 materials, via [Health Affairs press event PDF](https://www.healthaffairs.org/cms/10.1377/he20241230.971146/asset/nhe23-press-event-final.pdf)

## Verdict for C5: "driven largely by post-pandemic recovery spending and pharmaceutical costs"

**Verdict:** Unverified
**Confidence:** Low
**Provenance:** [inferred]
**Scrutiny:** [abstract]

There is partial support, but no source was found that attributes the 2019–2023 increase *largely* to these two factors. For 2023 specifically, CMS credits faster growth to "non-price factors, such as use and intensity of services" and to record insurance coverage (92.5% insured, driven by Marketplace enrollment). That fits loosely with "post-pandemic recovery," but CMS frames it as utilization and coverage. Retail prescription drug spending grew a fast 11.4% in 2023, to $449.7B, driven by diabetes and obesity drugs. Still, $449.7B is about 9% of the $4.9T total, so drugs alone cannot account for most of the growth. This rating was not upgraded to Accurate or Mostly accurate because the evidence only covers single-year 2023 drivers. A CMS or peer-reviewed breakdown of total growth from 2019 to 2023 by cause would be needed. Provenance is `[inferred]` rather than `[assumed]` because related CMS evidence was found; it just doesn't establish the claim.

**Sources:** CMS/Health Affairs NHE 2023 study (Dec. 2024); AHA and Association of Health Care Journalists summaries (Dec. 2024 / Jan. 2025)
**Citation:** "Retail prescription drug spending increased 11.4% to $449.7 billion in 2023, accelerating from a rate of 7.8% in 2022." — [Health Affairs, 2024](https://www.healthaffairs.org/doi/full/10.1377/forefront.20241217.924408)

## Claims Requiring Author Attention

- **C1** — The 2023 total is $4.9 trillion (CMS), not $4.3 trillion.
- **C2** — The 2023 GDP share is 17.6%; 17.3% was CMS's original 2022 figure (later revised to 17.4%).
- **C4** — The increase is real but bigger than the draft implies: about $1.1T (+29%), not about $0.5T. As a share of GDP, spending is roughly flat versus 2019.
- **C5** — No source supports this causal claim. CMS links 2023 growth mainly to utilization and intensity plus insurance coverage; drug spending grew fast but is about 9% of the total.
