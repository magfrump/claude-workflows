## Verdict for C1: "Oregon's SB 458 requires sellers to share appreciation with tenants who helped maintain the property."

**Verdict:** Inaccurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The 2021 Oregon SB 458 is a land-division law. It lets cities and counties divide lots that hold "middle housing" (duplexes, triplexes, townhouses and similar) so that each unit can sit on its own lot and be sold separately. It requires these divisions to go through the expedited land-division process in ORS 197.360–197.380, and it stops HOAs and restrictive covenants from banning such divisions. None of the sources describe anything about sellers sharing appreciation with tenants, and no source links SB 458 to tenant equity at all. Several sources agree on this: the Legislature's measure documents, DLCD's guidance, Oregon City's FAQ, and Habitat Oregon's summary. Two of these are primary (the bill record and DLCD guidance), which would normally support High confidence. Because I could only read them at `[abstract]` depth, confidence is capped at Medium. Reading the enrolled text (Oregon Laws 2021, ch. 103) in full would lift it to High.

**Sources:** Oregon Legislature, SB 458 (2021R1) measure document and staff measure summaries; DLCD, Guidance on SB 458 (2021–22); City of Oregon City, Middle Housing Land Division SB 458 FAQ; Habitat for Humanity of Oregon (2021).
**Citation:** "This bill allows the creation of fee-simple lots for newly constructed middle housing units that meet the local development code." (Oregon Legislature staff measure summary, as shown in search excerpt)

## Verdict for C2: "This bill was part of a broader package of housing reforms passed in the 2021 legislative session." (passage in 2021)

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

SB 458 belongs to the 2021 Regular Session (2021R1). It passed the Senate 25–4 and the House 54–2, Gov. Kate Brown signed it on May 26, 2021, and it became Oregon Laws 2021, Chapter 103. The legislative tracker and Habitat Oregon give the same facts. Confidence is capped at Medium because neither page could be opened past its search excerpt.

**Sources:** Oregon Legislature OLIS bill record, SB 458 (2021R1); Habitat for Humanity of Oregon press release (2021); LegiScan OR SB458 (2021).
**Citation:** "SB 458 passed with overwhelming bipartisan support from the Senate (25-4) and the House (54-2), and was signed by Gov. Brown on May 26." (Habitat Oregon, 2021)

## Verdict for C3: "This bill was part of a broader package of housing reforms passed in the 2021 legislative session." (part of a broader package)

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** inferred
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The 2021 session passed many housing bills, among them HB 2006 (emergency shelter siting), SB 8 (affordable housing without a zone change), and HB 2009 (mortgage deferral). The Legislature's research office (LPRO) published a "2021 Housing Legislative Summary Report" covering them, and a July 2021 City of Portland planning memo lists SB 458 among the session's land-use and housing bills. So the loose reading, "one of several housing bills passed in 2021," holds up. But I found no evidence that SB 458 was formally bundled into a package. Sources describe it as a standalone bill led by Habitat Oregon and the Oregon Home Builders Association to follow up on HB 2001 from 2019. The draft is accurate on the loose reading and unsupported on the stricter one. Two government documents agree here (the LPRO summary and the Portland memo), but the link between them is my inference and I saw both only as excerpts, so confidence is Medium.

**Sources:** Oregon LPRO, 2021 Housing Legislative Summary Report/Brief (2021); City of Portland planning memo (July 2021); Habitat for Humanity of Oregon (2021); Multnomah County legislative summary (2021).
**Citation:** "SB 458 builds upon the passage of HB 2001 in 2019, as well as the more than year-long rulemaking process by the Oregon Department of Land Conservation and Development." (Habitat Oregon, 2021)
