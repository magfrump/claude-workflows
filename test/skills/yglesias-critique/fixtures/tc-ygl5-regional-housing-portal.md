# One Front Door: A Shared Housing Help Portal for the Lakeshore Region

*White paper from the Lakeshore Housing Collaborative*

## Twenty-three doors, and all of them hard to find

A family facing eviction in the Lakeshore region today has to figure out, usually in a matter of days, which of dozens of programs they might qualify for. Emergency rental assistance is run separately by each of the four counties. Nine of the region's 23 municipalities operate their own small grant funds. Utility arrears help comes through two different nonprofits depending on which side of the county line you live on. Legal aid, landlord mediation, security-deposit loans and shelter placement each have their own intake forms, eligibility rules and phone lines.

Our member organizations see the consequences every week. A tenant in Ashby fills out a county application, waits three weeks, and learns she should have applied to the city fund instead. A father in Corriston is turned away from a shelter intake line because he called the wrong county's number. Caseworkers at our member agencies estimate they spend a third of their time simply figuring out where to send people.

The money is not the main problem. The region spent roughly $86 million last year on housing assistance across all of these programs. The problem is that people cannot find it.

## The vision

We propose One Front Door: a single regional website, phone line and walk-in intake process where any resident of the Lakeshore region can apply once and be matched to every program they qualify for.

A resident would answer one set of questions about household size, income, location and housing situation. The portal would screen them against the eligibility rules of every participating program, route the application to the right agencies automatically, and let the resident track its status in one place. Caseworkers at any participating agency would see the same record, so no one has to tell their story five times.

Similar coordinated-entry systems have been adopted in other parts of the country, and the technology is mature. Several vendors offer configurable case management platforms that could support the portal with modest customization.

## How we get there

We envision a collaborative approach that reflects the region's diversity and respects the autonomy of each jurisdiction.

- **A regional steering committee** made up of representatives from the four counties, interested municipalities, housing nonprofits, legal aid providers, landlords and people with lived experience of housing instability will guide the project's direction.
- **Shared funding** from participating jurisdictions and philanthropic partners, in proportion to their capacity, will cover an estimated $4.5 million in first-year build costs and $1.8 million a year in ongoing operations.
- **Common eligibility standards** will be developed by the steering committee over time, so that programs can gradually align their rules and paperwork.
- **Data sharing** among participating agencies will allow a single record to follow the resident across programs, with appropriate privacy safeguards.

Participation will be voluntary. We believe that once the first jurisdictions see the benefits, others will want to join.

## Timeline

- **Months 1 to 6:** Convene the steering committee, secure initial funding commitments and engage stakeholders.
- **Months 7 to 12:** Select a technology vendor and begin configuring the platform.
- **Months 13 to 18:** Launch a pilot with early-adopting jurisdictions.
- **Months 19 to 30:** Expand region-wide.

## Benefits

One Front Door will cut the time from first contact to assistance from weeks to days. It will reduce duplicate applications and the staff time spent processing them, freeing caseworkers to do actual casework. It will give the region, for the first time, a clear picture of how many households are seeking help, what they need and where the gaps are. And it will treat people in crisis with the dignity of a simple, coherent process.

## Conclusion

The Lakeshore region does not need more programs. It needs the programs it already has to work together. One Front Door is an achievable, relatively low-cost investment that would make every dollar the region already spends on housing assistance go further. We invite every county, municipality and partner organization to join us at the table.
