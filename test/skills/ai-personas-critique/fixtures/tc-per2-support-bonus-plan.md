# Support Team Compensation Redesign, H1 2027

**Author:** Director of Customer Support, Pellucid Software
**Audience:** VP Operations, People team, Support leads
**Status:** Proposal for approval at the November operations review

## Background

Pellucid's support organization has grown from 30 to 85 agents in two years, tracking the growth of our customer base. Over the same period our median time to resolution has drifted upward, from 19 hours to 31 hours, and the backlog of open tickets at the end of each week has nearly tripled. Customers notice. In the last two quarterly account reviews, "support responsiveness" was the most common complaint raised by enterprise customers, and two renewals were delayed while it was discussed.

Our current compensation is a flat salary with an annual merit increase decided by team leads. Leads tell us that the merit process is subjective, that high performers do not feel meaningfully rewarded, and that the difference between a strong year and an average one is about 1 percent of salary. Exit interviews from the eleven agents who left this year cite "no way to get ahead" more than any other reason.

We think both problems, slow resolution and weak differentiation, have the same fix: pay people for the work that matters most to customers, and make the rules transparent.

## Proposal

Starting in January, each agent's performance will be measured by a single, visible number: **tickets resolved per scheduled shift hour**, calculated weekly from the helpdesk and shown on a team leaderboard.

- Agents in the **top quartile** for the quarter receive a **$1,500 bonus**.
- Agents in the **second quartile** receive **$600**.
- Agents in the **bottom decile** for two consecutive quarters enter a performance improvement plan.

A ticket counts as resolved when the agent sets its status to Resolved. The metric is normalized by scheduled hours so that part-time agents and agents who spend time in training are not penalized. Tickets handled in the escalation queue count the same as front-line tickets, because we do not want agents to avoid hard work.

## Why a single number

We considered a balanced scorecard mixing resolution volume, customer satisfaction scores, quality audits, and peer feedback. We rejected it for three reasons.

1. **Legibility.** Agents told us in the spring survey that they want to know exactly what they are being measured on. A single number on a leaderboard answers that.
2. **Noise.** Customer satisfaction surveys have a 9 percent response rate, and a single angry respondent can swing an agent's quarterly score by several points. Quality audits cover only about 2 percent of tickets.
3. **Administrative cost.** Leads currently spend around four hours per agent per year on merit reviews. The new system is calculated automatically.

Satisfaction scores and quality audits will continue to be collected and reported to leads, but they will not affect pay.

## Expected impact

Based on the current spread between our fastest and median agents, we expect the median resolution rate to rise by about 20 percent within two quarters as agents adopt the habits of the top performers. At current volume that clears the standing backlog in roughly six weeks and brings median time to resolution back under 20 hours.

## Cost

With 85 agents, roughly 21 will land in the top quartile and 21 in the second quartile each quarter. That is about $44,000 per quarter, or $176,000 per year, which is less than the fully loaded cost of two additional agents. We believe the throughput gain is worth considerably more than two agents.

## Rollout

- **December:** Announce the plan at the all-hands; publish the metric definition and the leaderboard.
- **January:** First measurement quarter begins.
- **April:** First bonuses paid; review adoption with leads.
- **July:** Decide whether to extend the model to the Tier 2 technical team.

## Decision requested

Approve the compensation change for H1 2027 and the $88,000 bonus budget for the first two quarters.
