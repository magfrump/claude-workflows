# HomeStart: Putting Ownership Back Within Reach in Port Aldine

*Policy brief, Port Aldine Metropolitan Council, Housing Opportunity Committee*

## A generation locked out

In 2005, a Port Aldine household earning the regional median income could afford the median-priced home with a standard mortgage and a ten percent down payment. Today it cannot come close. The metro's median sale price has reached $540,000, while median household income has grown far more slowly. The result is a generation of teachers, nurses, electricians and bus drivers who grew up here, work here and want to stay here, but who have no realistic path to owning a home here.

Homeownership is not just a lifestyle preference. It is the main way middle-class families in this country build wealth. A family that buys at 30 instead of 40 has ten more years of equity growth, ten more years of stable housing costs, and a far better chance of passing something on to its children. When ownership is out of reach, inequality compounds across generations.

Our committee surveyed 1,900 renter households in the metro. Among those earning between 80 and 140 percent of area median income, 68 percent said they could afford a monthly mortgage payment on a starter home. What stops them is the down payment. At today's prices, a ten percent down payment on a $450,000 condominium is $45,000, and the typical renter household in our survey had saved about $14,000.

## The proposal

HomeStart will provide grants of $30,000 to first-time buyers in the seven-county Port Aldine metro who meet three conditions:

- household income between 80 and 140 percent of area median income;
- completion of an eight-hour homebuyer education course; and
- purchase of a primary residence priced at or below $600,000.

The program will fund 4,000 grants per year, for an annual cost of $120 million, financed by a dedicated quarter-cent increase in the regional sales tax. Grants will be structured as forgivable loans: one-fifth of the balance is forgiven for each year the buyer lives in the home, so a household that stays five years owes nothing.

Grants will be administered through participating mortgage lenders at closing, which means no new bureaucracy. Lenders already verify income and employment; they will simply add a HomeStart line to the closing statement and draw the funds from the Council's account.

## Why this works

Unlike most housing programs, HomeStart puts money directly in the hands of the families we are trying to help. It does not go to developers, it does not go to landlords, and it does not disappear into administrative overhead. Every dollar shows up at the closing table on behalf of a first-time buyer.

The program is also fast. Construction subsidies take years to produce a single unit. A down-payment grant can turn a renter into an owner in the time it takes to close a mortgage, which in our market is about 45 days.

Finally, HomeStart is fair. It is open to any qualifying household in any of the seven counties, whether they want to live near downtown or in the suburbs, in a condo or a single-family home.

## Market context

Port Aldine is one of the tightest housing markets in the country. The metro permits roughly 3,100 new homes a year, well below household formation, and the homeowner vacancy rate has hovered below one percent for six years. Homes listed at or below $600,000 typically receive multiple offers within the first week. Our first-time buyers are not failing because they are unprepared; they are losing bidding wars to buyers with more cash.

That is exactly why a down-payment grant is the right tool. It gives our families the cash they need to compete.

## Expected results

Over its first five years HomeStart will help 20,000 households become homeowners. Using the Council's wealth-building model, we estimate those households will accumulate $1.4 billion in combined home equity over the following decade. We expect the program to have an outsized effect on younger buyers and buyers of color, who are most likely to be held back by the down payment rather than by income.

## Conclusion

Port Aldine cannot let homeownership become a privilege of inheritance. HomeStart is simple, direct and fair. We recommend that the Council place the sales tax measure on the November ballot.
