# Keeping Stallbook Personal as We Go National

**From:** Co-founders, Stallbook
**To:** Board of directors
**Purpose:** Operating plan for the buyer and seller trust function during the national launch

## Where we are

Stallbook is a marketplace for handmade goods from independent makers: ceramics, textiles, woodwork, small-batch food. We launched in three regional markets two years ago. Today we have about 1,400 active sellers and 26,000 buyers who placed at least one order in the last ninety days.

Our buyers and sellers tell us, over and over, that what makes Stallbook different is that a person looks at every problem. When an order arrives broken, or never arrives, or does not match the listing, the buyer opens a dispute and one of our three trust specialists reads the whole history, messages both sides, looks at the photos, and makes a decision. A typical dispute takes about 45 minutes of specialist time across its life. We receive about 40 disputes a week, and the team resolves them within two business days.

This is our strongest brand asset. In our last seller survey, 82 percent of sellers named "fair, human dispute handling" as a reason they list with us rather than with larger marketplaces. Buyer reviews mention it constantly. Our dispute reversal rate on appeal is under 3 percent.

## The national launch

With the Series A closed, we are opening Stallbook to buyers and sellers nationally in March. Our plan, which the board approved in September, targets 50 times our current active buyer base within eighteen months of launch. Marketing spend, seller onboarding and logistics partnerships are all sized to that target, and the finance model assumes order volume grows in proportion to active buyers.

## What we propose for trust

We propose to keep the model that got us here, unchanged, through the launch period.

- **Every dispute is decided by a person.** We will not introduce automated rulings, auto-refund rules, or dispute-deflection flows. Buyers and sellers should never feel they are arguing with a bot.
- **The same three specialists.** Our trust team knows our sellers, our categories and our policies better than anyone we could hire. Continuity matters more than headcount in a function built on judgment and trust. The founders will cover specialist vacations personally, as we do now.
- **The same service standard.** Every dispute gets a decision within two business days.
- **No outsourcing.** We have spoken to two trust-and-safety outsourcing firms and do not believe either could match our quality or tone.

We will invest instead in tooling for the existing team: a better internal dashboard that pulls order history, message threads and photos into one view, which the team estimates will save about five minutes per dispute.

## Why not change now

Changing the trust model at the same moment we launch nationally would put our most valuable asset at risk exactly when new customers are forming their first impression. The larger marketplaces we compete with are known for opaque, automated dispute handling; our human approach is how we win sellers away from them. If we are going to adjust the model, we would rather do it after the launch has stabilized, informed by real data from national customers.

## Budget

Trust team salaries stay at their current level. The dashboard work is one engineer for six weeks. We are requesting no additional budget for the trust function in the launch plan.

## What we ask of the board

Endorse the principle that trust and dispute handling remain fully human and in-house, with the current team, for the eighteen months following national launch.
