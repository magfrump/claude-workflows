## Verdict for C1: "Nearly 70% of parents spend a fifth of their income on childcare."

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed (that the figure exists in a survey) + inferred (applying it to "parents" in general)
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The figure appears to come from Care.com's 2023 Cost of Care Report. Of about 3,000 surveyed parents, 67% said they spend 20% or more of household income on childcare. "Nearly 70%" rounds 67% up slightly. The bigger issue is scope. The draft states this as a fact about parents in general, but it describes one company's survey of its respondents, and Care.com's release says the results were not weighted ("no post-stratification has been applied"). A nationally representative federal survey, the 2019 National Survey of Early Care and Education (NSECE), found 12% of all households with children spending over 20% of income on childcare. NSECE counts households that pay nothing for care, so the two numbers measure different groups rather than contradicting each other. Confidence is Medium because there is one primary source. It is capped at Medium regardless, because I could only read search-result summaries: every attempt to open the full pages failed with DNS errors. Reading the 2023 Care.com report in full would confirm the exact wording and how the sample was recruited.

**Sources:** Care.com / BusinessWire, "New Care.com Report Reveals Soaring Childcare Costs…" (June 13, 2023); BenefitsPro (Aug. 2023); CNBC (Aug. 7, 2023); NSECE 2019 via Research Connections.
**Citation:** "Two-thirds of families, 67%, spend 20% or more of their household income on child care, according to a recent report by Care.com that surveyed 3,000 parents."

## Verdict for C2: "This finding, consistent across multiple surveys"

**Verdict:** Inaccurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** inferred
**Scrutiny:** abstract

I found only one survey with a figure near 70%: Care.com's 2023 report. Care.com's own surveys in other years give quite different numbers. About 31% of parents reported spending 20% or more in 2019, 51% in 2022, and 60% in 2024 (the 2024 figure comes from Kiplinger and Audacy coverage, not Care.com's own page). The 2025 and 2026 reports give averages (22% and 20% of income) rather than a share above 20%. The federal NSECE puts the share at 12% of all households with children. How I got there: the 67% figure is from one survey-year; the same survey's other years range from 31% to 60%; and an independent federal survey is far lower. So the evidence does not show a consistent ~70% finding across surveys. The two sources (Care.com and NSECE) are independent primary sources, but I only saw summaries of both, so confidence is capped at Medium. Normally an Inaccurate verdict calls for reading the full sources, but the page fetches failed. Opening the 2022 and 2024 Care.com reports and the NSECE brief would confirm the year-by-year numbers. One search-tool summary labeled the 67% figure as 2024 data. The original BusinessWire release is dated June 2023, so I treated it as 2023.

**Sources:** Care.com 2023 report (BusinessWire, June 2023); Bloomberg (June 15, 2022) and First Five Years Fund (June 2022) on the 2022 report; Care.com 2024 report (BusinessWire, Jan. 17, 2024) with Kiplinger and Audacy coverage; Care.com 2025 and 2026 reports; NSECE 2019 (Research Connections).
**Citation:** "Of the respondents, 60% are spending 20% or more of their income on childcare, while 84% are spending 10% or more." (Kiplinger, on Care.com's 2024 report)

---

## Claims Requiring Author Attention

- **C1** — The figure (67%, rounded to "nearly 70%") comes from one unweighted Care.com survey from 2023. The draft presents it as a fact about parents in general. Federal NSECE data shows 12% of all households with children spending more than 20% on care.
- **C2** — The "consistent across multiple surveys" claim isn't supported. Other Care.com years show 31% (2019), 51% (2022) and 60% (2024), and the federal NSECE shows 12%. I found no other survey near 70%.
