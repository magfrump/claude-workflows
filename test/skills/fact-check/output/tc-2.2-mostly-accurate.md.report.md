## Verdict for C1: "Nearly 70% of parents spend a fifth of their income on childcare."

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** inferred
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The figure seems to come from Care.com's 2023 Cost of Care Report, a survey of about 3,000 parents. Coverage of that report says 67% of families spend 20% or more of household income on child care, which fits "nearly 70%." But the figure is outdated and narrower than the draft says:

- **Later editions are lower.** The 2024 Care.com report (2,000 parents) put the share at 60%. The 2026 report gives an *average* spend of 20%, down from 22% (2025), 24% (2024) and 27% (2023).
- **Earlier editions were lower too:** 51% in 2022 and about 31% in 2019.
- **It's a survey of Care.com parents, not all parents.** The respondents pay for care. "Parents" in general would include the many who pay nothing. An older Pew analysis of Census data put child care at 7.2% of family income for families paying for care (2014), though it measures something different.

**Why Medium and not High:** all of this comes from convergent secondary coverage, read only as search summaries. Opening the 2023 and 2024 Care.com reports would confirm the exact wording and sample.

**Sources:** Care.com Cost of Care Reports, 2023 and 2024, via BenefitsPro (2023), CNBC (2023), Yahoo Life (2023) and Business Wire (2024); Care.com 2026 Cost of Care Report; Bloomberg / First Five Years Fund (2022); Pew Research Center (2014)
**Citation:** https://benefitspro.com/2023/08/08/two-thirds-of-parents-spend-20-of-income-on-child-care-as-costs-continue-to-soar

## Verdict for C2: "This financial burden is the primary reason many families with young children have one parent exit the workforce entirely."

**Verdict:** Unverified
**Confidence:** Low
**Provenance:** inferred (that cost is *one* reason parents leave work); assumed (that it is the *primary* reason)
**Scrutiny:** abstract

There is evidence that cost is *a* reason parents leave work:

- A 2022 Fortune report on the Census Household Pulse Survey said just over a third of women with school-age kids who left their jobs cited child care being too expensive.
- An earlier Care.com report (the 9th annual edition) found 21% of parents had left or planned to leave the workforce to care for their children.
- Pew (2014) said rising child care costs "may help explain" the rise in stay-at-home mothers.

I found no source that ranks cost as the *primary* reason. Some sources measure caregiving or access instead of cost. For example, a LendingTree analysis of Pulse data found 10.7% of non-retired, non-working adults cited caring for children, without isolating cost. A Federal Reserve report found that over half of two-parent families with one parent at home used no child care at all.

**What would settle it:** a representative survey (e.g., Census CPS or Pulse microdata, or a Pew or Federal Reserve survey) that asks stay-at-home parents their main reason and reports cost as the top answer.

**Sources:** Fortune (2022, on Census Household Pulse data); Care.com 9th annual Cost of Care Report (via search summary); Pew Research Center (2014); LendingTree analysis of Household Pulse data; Federal Reserve, *Economic Well-Being of U.S. Households in 2024* (2025); Center for American Progress analysis of 2021 Pulse data
**Citation:** https://fortune.com/2022/08/18/parents-with-young-children-juggling-quitting-work-childcare-issues/

---

## Claims Requiring Author Attention

- **C1** — The ~67% figure is from Care.com's 2023 survey. The 2024 edition shows 60%, and the sample is parents who pay for care, not all parents. The figure needs a named source and year.
- **C2** — No source found showing cost is the *primary* reason a parent leaves the workforce. The evidence shows only that it's one reason among several, cited by roughly a fifth to a third of respondents depending on the survey.
