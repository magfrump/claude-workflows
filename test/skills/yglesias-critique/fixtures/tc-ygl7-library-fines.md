# Fine Free Fenwick: Why the Library Should Stop Charging Overdue Fines

*Memo to the Fenwick Public Library Board of Trustees from the Library Director*

The Fenwick Public Library charges 25 cents a day for overdue books, up to a $10 cap per item. I recommend that the board eliminate overdue fines on all materials starting July 1, while keeping replacement charges for items that are lost or damaged.

Our goal as a library is to get books into the hands of as many residents as possible. Overdue fines work against that goal. Today 6,400 cardholders, about 14 percent of the total, have borrowing blocked because they owe more than $5. Those blocked accounts are concentrated in the three branches that serve our lowest-income neighborhoods, where about one in four cardholders is blocked. Staff at those branches report that parents regularly tell them they stopped letting their children borrow books because they were afraid of fines.

Fines are also a poor source of revenue. We collected $61,000 in overdue fines last year, less than one percent of the operating budget. Collecting them has a cost: circulation staff spend time processing small payments and handling disputes at the desk, and the finance office reconciles cash at eight locations every day. Our business manager estimates that staff time spent on fines is worth about $38,000 a year, so the net revenue is closer to $23,000.

Fines do not do much to get books returned. Library systems that have gone fine free have generally reported little or no change in on-time returns, and some reported that long-overdue items came back once patrons no longer faced a bill. We will keep the mechanism that actually works: an item more than 30 days overdue is treated as lost and billed at replacement cost, and that charge is cleared when the item comes back.

The $23,000 in net revenue can be absorbed within the materials budget without reducing purchases, by delaying a planned shelving replacement at the Main branch by one year.

If the board approves, staff will clear existing overdue fine balances on July 1, unblock affected accounts, update the website and notices, and report back at the December meeting on circulation, returns and the number of reactivated cardholders.

I ask the board to approve the change at its June meeting.
