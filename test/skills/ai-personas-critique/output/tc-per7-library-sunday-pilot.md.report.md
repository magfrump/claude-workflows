## Persona Critiques

### The Empiricist: What evidence actually supports this claim?

User goal: stress-test this proposal from several independent angles before it goes to the people who will decide on it.
Current task: Apply the Empiricist lens — what evidence actually supports this claim? — to this proposal.
Success criterion: A 100-200 word critique grounded in proposal specifics, with severity and a test/mitigation.

The main success metric can be met through cannibalization. "Sunday visits ≥ 60% of Saturday visits in the same weeks" improves if Saturday regulars simply move to Sunday. Saturday falls, Sunday rises, and the ratio looks great with zero new demand. The proposal's own evidence points the same way: Saturday already runs 40% above weekday average, so the most likely Sunday visitors are existing Saturday patrons.

The survey evidence is also weaker than it looks. "Weekend hours" is the most-wanted change at a branch that is already open Saturdays. It may mean *longer Saturday hours*, not Sunday hours.

The exit survey, offered at the desk, will reach only visitors who approach the desk and will self-select. "Could you have come at another time?" invites a socially convenient "no."

Finally, the "more than a mile from any branch" metric measures a different population from the one used to justify Eastgate, which is residents within walking distance.

**Severity:** Significant weakness. The decision threshold cannot distinguish new demand from shifted demand.
**Test/mitigation:**
- Replace the ratio with *total weekend visits* (Saturday + Sunday) compared against the same weeks last year.
- Track whether Sunday cardholders also visited on a Saturday in the prior 90 days.
- Hand the survey to every Nth visitor at the door.
- Add a metric for cards registered at the two apartment complexes.

### The End User Advocate: Would a real person actually use this, and how?

User goal: stress-test this proposal from several independent angles before it goes to the people who will decide on it.
Current task: Apply the End User Advocate lens — would a real person actually use this, and how? — to this proposal.
Success criterion: A 100-200 word critique grounded in proposal specifics, with severity and a test/mitigation.

The target user is a resident of the east-side apartment complexes who can't make current hours. That person mostly *isn't in the library now*, so in-branch signage reaches the wrong people. The proposal assumes "word gets around" but names no outreach to the complexes. Walking-distance patrons also bear the full cost of February weather, which will depress early Sundays more than at a branch people drive to.

A new routine typically takes several repetitions to form. Twelve weeks, with the decision resting on weeks 9–12, leaves little runway. The window also likely includes Easter Sunday (March 28, 2027), which will dent one of the counted weeks or the run-up to them.

It's also unclear what a Sunday visitor can actually *do*. Are holds pickup, computer help, and children's areas available? With a small Sunday crew, a visitor who arrives for help with a job application and finds limited service may not return.

**Severity:** Significant weakness. Low turnout from poor reach would be read as low demand.
**Test/mitigation:**
- Budget a small outreach effort before launch: flyers or contact with the complexes' property managers, plus messages in common languages.
- State which services run on Sundays.
- Note holiday Sundays in advance and exclude them from the threshold average.

### The Systems Thinker: What feedback loops and emergent effects does this create?

User goal: stress-test this proposal from several independent angles before it goes to the people who will decide on it.
Current task: Apply the Systems Thinker lens — what feedback loops and emergent effects does this create? — to this proposal.
Success criterion: A 100-200 word critique grounded in proposal specifics, with severity and a test/mitigation.

"No other branch's hours change" describes hours, not effects.

**Spillover to other branches.** Patrons of other branches may drive to Eastgate on Sundays. That shifts load away from where the measured demand lives, and it may also inflate the "far from a branch" metric.

**Precedent.** A successful pilot creates a reinforcing loop. Other branches' patrons and board members will ask "why not us?" The real policy question becomes system-wide Sunday service, which this proposal never prices.

**Staffing.** Paying four volunteers a premium rate sets a precedent under the staff agreement. If Sunday becomes permanent, "volunteer" coverage tends to become an expectation, and that can affect morale or bargaining.

**Hard to reverse.** Signage giving notice of a trial helps, but closing a service people have started using generates complaints regardless of notice. That biases the April decision toward continuing even if the thresholds are missed.

**Timing.** Twelve weeks from February 7 ends April 25. If the April board meeting falls before then, the "final four weeks" data won't be complete.

**Severity:** Significant weakness on the timing and precedent points. The rest are points to consider.
**Test/mitigation:**
- Confirm the April meeting date against the pilot end, or move the decision to May.
- Track Sunday visitors' home branch.
- Brief the board up front that this pilot is a test case for system-wide policy.

### The Opportunity Cost Accountant: What are we NOT doing by pursuing this?

User goal: stress-test this proposal from several independent angles before it goes to the people who will decide on it.
Current task: Apply the Opportunity Cost Accountant lens — what are we NOT doing by pursuing this? — to this proposal.
Success criterion: A 100-200 word critique grounded in proposal specifics, with severity and a test/mitigation.

The pilot itself is cheap and well bounded, which is a real strength. The concern is what it commits the board to next.

**What the programming money isn't doing.** The proposal treats $11,800 "left at the end of the last quarter" as free money. It doesn't say whether spring programs (story times, ESL, summer-reading preparation) were planned against it, or why it went unspent. Spending a third of it on hours means that much less programming at the same branch.

**The cost of continuing.** Continuing year-round costs about $18,500 a year (52 × $355), more than the entire remaining programming line. No recurring funding source is named. A "continue" vote in April is therefore really a budget decision the proposal hasn't framed.

**The cheaper alternative.** If the survey's "weekend hours" demand is partly for longer Saturdays, extending Saturday by two hours might capture much of the benefit at lower cost and with no new day to staff.

**Severity:** Point to consider for the pilot itself. Significant weakness for the continuation decision.
**Test/mitigation:**
- Confirm nothing planned is displaced from the programming line.
- Add the annualized cost and a candidate funding source to the April report.
- Name extended Saturday hours as the comparison alternative.

### Convergent Findings
- **Shifted demand vs. new demand.** The Empiricist (cannibalization of the Saturday ratio, "weekend hours" ambiguity), the Systems Thinker (spillover from other branches), and the Opportunity Cost Accountant (the extended-Saturday alternative) all independently doubt that the pilot can show *new* demand from the intended population. This is the highest-signal finding.
- **The pilot is cheap, but the continuation decision is under-specified.** The Systems Thinker (precedent, hard to reverse, timing) and the Opportunity Cost Accountant (about $18.5k a year with no named funding source) both say the April decision carries more weight than the proposal acknowledges.
- **The target population isn't reached or measured directly.** The End User Advocate (no outreach to the complexes) and the Empiricist (the "more than a mile" metric doesn't match the walking-distance rationale) agree that the people Eastgate was chosen for aren't clearly reached or counted.

### Tensions
- **Clean measurement vs. fair trial.** The End User Advocate wants outreach and ramp time, and would exclude holiday weeks, to give Sunday a fair chance. The Empiricist wants measurement that doesn't flatter the result. Both can be satisfied by doing outreach *and* measuring against a prior-year total-weekend baseline, but the board should see that the current thresholds are both too easy (the ratio rewards cannibalization) and potentially too harsh (low reach and February weather).
- **Cheap to try vs. hard to undo.** The Opportunity Cost Accountant rates the pilot itself as low-risk. The Systems Thinker notes that once Sundays exist, stopping carries political cost. The pilot is financially reversible but not fully socially reversible.
