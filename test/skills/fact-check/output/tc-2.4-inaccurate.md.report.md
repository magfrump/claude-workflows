## Verdict for C1: "France banned homeschooling in 2021"

**Verdict:** Inaccurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** [observed]
**Scrutiny:** [abstract]

France did not ban homeschooling. Law no. 2021-1109 of 24 August 2021 replaced the old system, where families simply declared they were homeschooling, with one where they must get permission first. The new system took effect at the start of the 2022 school year, not in 2021. Permission is granted on four grounds: health or disability; intensive sports or artistic training; a travelling family or distance from a school; and a "situation specific to the child" behind the education plan. Children already homeschooled got automatic permission for 2022–23 and 2023–24. The Conseil constitutionnel upheld the system (Decision 2021-823 DC, 13 August 2021) with a condition on how the last ground is applied. It did not treat the law as abolishing homeschooling. Critics call it a de facto or "in principle" ban, but that is how they describe it, not what the law says. One more detail: Macron's October 2020 announcement proposed allowing homeschooling only for medical reasons. Parliament widened that before passing the law.

Confidence: two independent primary sources (the education ministry and the Conseil constitutionnel decision) would normally support High. But both were seen only through search summaries, so the rating stops at Medium. Reading the full decision (around paragraphs 70–76) and the full ministry page would lift it.

**Sources:** French Ministry of National Education, "L'instruction dans la famille" (education.gouv.fr); Conseil constitutionnel, Décision n° 2021-823 DC (2021); Assemblée nationale written answers on Loi n° 2021-1109 (2022–23); Café pédagogique, 24 Aug 2021

## Verdict for C2: "as part of President Macron's campaign against separatism"

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** [inferred]
**Scrutiny:** [abstract]

The homeschooling rules are in the "loi confortant le respect des principes de la République," which is widely called the "loi séparatisme" (vie-publique.fr files it under that name). Macron first announced the homeschooling limits in his 2 October 2020 speech against separatism at Les Mureaux. The reasoning: AFP coverage at the time and an analysis by the University of Montpellier both describe the speech as tying homeschooling to separatism, and the law that resulted carries the homeschooling provisions. That makes two secondary sources that agree, which supports Medium. The Élysée transcript is the primary source but couldn't be opened. Reading it would lift the rating.

**Sources:** AFP dispatch, 5 Oct 2020 (via ac-normandie press review); Weka/AFP (2020); Université de Montpellier, "Instruction à domicile : de quoi parle-t-on ?"; vie-publique.fr law page; Élysée, speech of 2 Oct 2020 (title only)
**Citation:** [source: AFP dispatch, 5 October 2020, headline "Séparatismes : l'instruction scolaire à domicile sera strictement limitée, annonce Macron"]

## Verdict for C3: "This made France the first major Western democracy to eliminate the practice entirely"

**Verdict:** Inaccurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** [inferred]
**Scrutiny:** [abstract]

This fails for two reasons. First, C1 shows France did not eliminate homeschooling. Second, Germany has banned homeschooling as an alternative to school for decades. Compulsory schooling dates from the Weimar era (1919), and the 1938 Reichsschulpflichtgesetz is usually cited as the law that removed the remaining exemptions. Sources differ on the exact start date (1918, 1919 or 1938), but all agree it came long before 2021. The European Court of Human Rights accepted Germany's ban in *Konrad v. Germany* (application no. 35504/03, decision of 11 September 2006), and Germany's Constitutional Court upheld it again in 2014. Sweden's 2010 Education Act also allows homeschooling only for "exceptional reasons" (*synnerliga skäl*), applied very narrowly. So under any reading, France was not first.

Confidence: the ECtHR decision (primary, seen in summary) plus Spiegler's academic overview (secondary) support Medium. Reading the Konrad decision in full would lift it.

**Sources:** ECtHR, *Konrad v. Germany*, no. 35504/03 (2006); Spiegler, "Home Education in Germany: An Overview of the Contemporary Situation" (via UNICAMP); Wikipedia, "Schulpflicht"; Swedish Supreme Administrative Court, HFD 2013 ref. 49
**Citation:** [source: European Court of Human Rights, *Konrad v. Germany*, Application no. 35504/03, inadmissibility decision of 11 September 2006]
