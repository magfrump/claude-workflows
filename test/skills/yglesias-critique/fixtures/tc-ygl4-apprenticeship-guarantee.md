# A Pathway for Every Graduate: Scaling the Keystone Apprenticeship Model Statewide

*Proposal from Keystone Pathways to the State Workforce Development Board*

## The problem

Every June, tens of thousands of young people in this state walk across a graduation stage with no plan for what comes next. Roughly 45,000 graduating seniors each year do not enroll in college in the fall. Many of them drift through a series of short-term, low-wage jobs, and by their mid-twenties a large share are neither working steadily nor in school. We know how to change that trajectory, because we have done it.

## What we built

Keystone Pathways launched in the city of Dunmore Falls five years ago with a simple idea: pair high school seniors who are not headed to college with local employers for a paid, twelve-month apprenticeship in a skilled trade or technical field, with a dedicated coach who stays with the student the whole year.

Over five cohorts we have enrolled 320 young people with 74 partner employers: machine shops, electrical contractors, hospital systems, logistics firms and a regional utility. The results have exceeded our expectations:

- 88 percent of apprentices completed the full twelve months;
- 71 percent were hired into permanent jobs by their host employer;
- average starting wages for those hired were $21.40 per hour, compared with $13.10 for a comparison group of local graduates; and
- 92 percent of participating employers said they would host another apprentice.

Our participants applied through a competitive process that included an essay, two interviews and a reference from a teacher or counselor. We accepted about one in three applicants, and we are proud that every one of them earned their spot.

The fully loaded cost per participant, including wage subsidies to employers for the first three months, coaching and program administration, has averaged $6,200.

## The proposal

We propose that the state guarantee a Keystone-model apprenticeship to every graduating senior who is not enrolling in college and wants one, beginning with the class graduating two years from now.

At 45,000 eligible graduates and our demonstrated cost of $6,200 per participant, a full guarantee would cost about $279 million a year. Even if only half of eligible graduates take it up, the state would be investing roughly $140 million a year in a program with a proven track record.

Keystone Pathways would serve as the statewide intermediary, licensing our model, curriculum and coaching framework to regional workforce boards, which would recruit employers and hire coaches in their areas. We have already documented our processes in a 140-page operations manual so that the model can be replicated faithfully.

## Why a guarantee

Pilots are valuable, but they help only the lucky few. A guarantee changes expectations for an entire generation. When every student knows that a real apprenticeship is waiting for them, counselors can plan with them from ninth grade, and students who might otherwise disengage have a reason to finish strong. A guarantee also sends a signal to employers that the state is serious about building a skilled workforce.

## The return on investment

Our hired apprentices earn about $8.30 an hour more than comparable graduates. Over a full working year that is roughly $17,000 in additional earnings, generating new income and sales tax revenue and reducing reliance on public assistance. Our economic consultant estimates that the program pays for itself within four years of each participant's completion.

Scaled statewide, the same results would mean more than 28,000 additional young people in stable, well-paid careers every year.

## Conclusion

We know this model works because we have watched it work, one student and one employer at a time, for five years. The only thing standing between our results and every young person in this state is the decision to scale it. We ask the Board to include the Apprenticeship Guarantee in its budget request to the legislature.
