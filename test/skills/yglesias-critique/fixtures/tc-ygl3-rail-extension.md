# Finish the Line: The Case for Fully Funding the Eastline Extension

*Tri-County Transit Authority, Office of the General Manager — memo to the Board and the Regional Funding Task Force*

## Where we are

Twenty years ago this region made a bet on rail. The Westline opened in 2004: eleven miles, fourteen stations, built for $1.9 billion. It now carries 61,000 riders on an average weekday, and the neighborhoods along it have added more jobs and housing than any other part of the tri-county area. By almost any measure it is the most successful public investment the region has made in a generation.

The east side has been waiting ever since. The Eastline Extension would run 9.2 miles from Central Station to the Marlow Park employment center, with eleven stations, mostly underground through the dense Riverside and Holbrook neighborhoods. Our ridership model projects 48,000 weekday riders by the tenth year of service. For the residents of Holbrook, where 40 percent of households do not own a car, the Eastline would cut the typical commute to Marlow Park from 70 minutes by bus to 24 minutes by train.

## The budget

The current engineering estimate for the Eastline is $7.8 billion in year-of-expenditure dollars, including a 30 percent contingency. That figure has grown from $4.1 billion at the time of the 2016 alternatives analysis.

We want to be candid with the Board about why. Much of the growth reflects decisions made to do this project right. The alignment moved underground through Riverside after community concerns about noise and visual impact on the historic district. Station footprints were enlarged to meet updated accessibility and fire-life-safety standards. We added a second mezzanine at four stations at the request of the city. We expanded our environmental mitigation commitments, and our utility relocation estimate grew after more detailed surveys. Construction labor and materials costs in the region have also risen sharply.

For comparison, the Westline cost roughly $3.2 billion in today's dollars. Some have pointed out that the Eastline is shorter and costs more than twice as much. That comparison ignores twenty years of higher standards, a more demanding tunnel alignment and a more complicated urban environment. We are building a better railroad than we built in 2004, and better costs more.

## The funding gap

Federal capital grants and existing local revenues cover about $4.6 billion. The remaining $3.2 billion is the gap this memo asks the Task Force to close. We recommend a half-cent regional sales tax increase for thirty years, which our financial advisors estimate would support $3.4 billion in bond proceeds, enough to close the gap with a modest reserve.

We considered and rejected phasing the project, for example by opening the first four miles to Holbrook and deferring the rest. Phasing would add remobilization costs, delay the ridership benefits at Marlow Park, and risk leaving the extension permanently unfinished.

## Delivery

The Authority will deliver the Eastline using the same approach that succeeded on the Westline: a design-bid-build model, with our general engineering consultant preparing the designs and managing the construction contracts. Our current general engineering consultant team has been with the project since 2016 and knows it better than anyone. We will expand the Authority's project oversight staff from 38 to 55 positions to manage the larger program.

## Why now

Every year of delay adds roughly $300 million to the cost through escalation alone. Federal grant commitments are available in the current funding cycle and may not be available in the next one. And the east side has already waited twenty years for a promise the region made when the Westline opened.

## What we are asking

We ask the Task Force to endorse the half-cent sales tax for the November ballot and to commit the region to funding the Eastline in full at the current estimate. A fully funded project gives contractors the certainty they need to bid competitively and gives the Authority the stability to deliver on schedule.

The Westline showed what this region can do when it commits. Let's finish the line.
