# PawSlot — Seed Round Memo

**Raising:** $3.5M seed · **Stage:** 140 paying clinics, 11 months after launch

## The problem

Independent veterinary clinics still book most appointments by phone. A typical
three-vet practice has one or two front-desk staff who spend a large share of
the day answering calls, confirming appointments and chasing no-shows. Pet
owners want to book at 10pm from their phone, and they increasingly pick the
clinic that lets them. The large practice-management systems that clinics run
on were built for billing and medical records. Their booking modules, where they
exist at all, are desktop-only and require the owner to call anyway to confirm.

## The product

PawSlot is a mobile-first booking page and reminder service for independent
clinics. The clinic embeds our widget on its website and Google profile. Owners
see real open slots, book in under a minute, and receive text reminders at 72
and 24 hours. Clinics get a daily schedule view and a no-show report. Setup takes
about two hours with our onboarding team, and we sync one-way into the clinic's
calendar. Across our first 140 clinics, phone volume at the front desk fell by
roughly a third and no-shows fell from 9% to 5%.

## Why we win: our moat

Our moat is that we got here first. Nobody else has built a booking product
designed specifically for independent veterinary clinics, and we believe we
have an 18-month head start on anyone who tries. Every month we spend in the
market widens that lead: our team knows the workflows of vet front desks better
than any generalist scheduling company ever will, our brand is becoming the name
clinics hear at regional conferences, and our product roadmap is informed by
hundreds of hours of clinic interviews that a new entrant would have to repeat.

We think of this as a land-grab. There are about 26,000 independent clinics in
the country, and the first company to sign most of them will own the category.
Speed is the moat. Once we are the default, latecomers will be fighting for
scraps. That is why most of this round goes to sales and onboarding capacity
rather than to new product surface.

## Distribution

We sell through two channels. First, a partnership program with state
veterinary medical associations, which promote us in member newsletters and at
their annual meetings in exchange for a member discount; we have signed four
states and have conversations open with nine more. Second, a small inside-sales
team that works inbound leads from those events and from clinic owners who see
the widget at a neighbouring practice. Our plan is priced at $189 per month per
location, which suits a short sales cycle: the median clinic signs within 16 days
of a first call, and our reps handle the demo and onboarding by video.

We have avoided paid search so far. Our association channel produces leads at a
fraction of what we see quoted for local-services keywords, and we would rather
put the money into onboarding quality.

## Retention

Monthly logo churn has averaged 1.4% since launch. Clinics that stay past 90
days almost never leave. We read this as a product that has become part of the
daily routine at the front desk.

## Competition

General-purpose scheduling tools exist, but they do not understand multi-vet
calendars, exam-room constraints or species-specific appointment lengths, and
clinics that try them usually give up. Practice-management vendors have booking
modules, but those are afterthoughts. We do not expect the large vendors to
prioritise consumer-facing booking in the near term; their roadmaps are focused
on cloud migration of their records products.

## Use of funds and milestones

- Grow the sales and onboarding team from 6 to 18 people.
- Sign 20 additional state associations.
- Reach 1,000 paying clinics within 18 months of close.
- Ship two-way sync with the three most common practice-management systems.

## The ask

We are raising $3.5M to press our head start while the window is open. The
category will have a winner, and we intend to be it before anyone else realises
the category exists.
