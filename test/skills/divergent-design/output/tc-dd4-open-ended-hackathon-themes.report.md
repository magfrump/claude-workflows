## Themes that need all three groups

Each one is hard to win without engineers, designers and support staff on the same team.

**1. "Fix the Thing Customers Complain About Most"**
Support staff bring the most common tickets, workarounds and painful conversations. Teams pick one and remove it. Support knows the problem, design reshapes the experience and engineering builds the fix.
- *Prompt:* "Make one ticket category disappear."

**2. "A Day in the Life"**
Teams shadow a real user type, like a new customer, an admin, a frustrated user or a support agent, and build something for that person.
- *Prompt:* "Save [persona] 10 minutes a day."

**3. "Our Own Worst Tools"**
Look inward at internal tools, processes, onboarding and dashboards. Everyone has opinions, and support staff often have the worst tools.
- *Prompt:* "Build the tool you wish you had on your first day."

**4. "Reverse Hackathon / Break It"**
Teams try to break, confuse or misuse your own product, then present the best fix along with what they found.
- *Prompt:* "Find the most surprising way a customer could get stuck."

**5. "First Five Minutes"**
Focus only on the new-user experience: signup, the first screen, the first "aha" moment.
- *Prompt:* "Get a brand-new user to value twice as fast."

**6. "Accessibility & Inclusion"**
Make the product usable for people with low vision, people on slow connections, non-native speakers and screen-reader users. It's very cross-functional and builds empathy.
- *Prompt:* "Use our product with one sense or ability taken away, then fix what hurts."

**7. "No Code Allowed" (or "Code Optional")**
Solutions can be a process, a help article, a redesigned email or a prototype in Figma. This keeps non-engineers from feeling like spectators.
- *Prompt:* "Solve a real problem without shipping to production."

**8. "Time Travel"**
"It's 2030. What does our product look like?" Teams build a demo or a convincing fake of the future. It's playful and visionary, and good for a first event.

**9. "Delight"**
Skip the roadmap and build something that makes users or coworkers smile: easter eggs, micro-interactions, better error messages, celebration moments.
- *Prompt:* "Make someone say 'oh, that's nice.'"

**10. "AI in the Workflow"**
Look for places where AI could help customers or staff: ticket triage, a smarter help center, auto-summaries, design assistants. It's timely, but put a "must solve a real, named pain point" rule on it so it doesn't turn into a gimmick contest.

## Formats that mix people

- **Umbrella theme with tracks:** One headline theme, like "Remove Friction," with tracks for Customers, Colleagues and Wildcard. It's broad enough for everyone and still gives some focus.
- **Problem marketplace:** Before the event, anyone can post a problem, and support staff especially should be encouraged to. Teams form around problems instead of around friends.
- **Required team mix:** For example, each team of 4–6 needs at least one person from support, one from design and one from engineering, with no more than two from the same team.
- **Mystery pairing:** Randomly pair people across departments for a short warm-up challenge before team formation.

## Ideas for a first hackathon

- **Prizes that reward collaboration:** "Best cross-team collaboration," "Most customer impact," "Best non-code solution" and "Most likely to ship." These should sit alongside "Best overall."
- **Short demos:** 3 minutes each, so presentation skill doesn't decide the winner.
- **Collect problems now:** A quick survey asking "What's one thing that annoys you or our customers?" gives you a backlog and builds interest.
- **A follow-up promise:** Commit up front to actually doing something with one or two winners. That's the main thing that makes people sign up for the second hackathon.

## Starting points if you want them

These are only my suggestions, since I didn't have your input:
- **Most impact for a first event:** #1 (Fix the Thing Customers Complain About Most) as the umbrella, with a problem marketplace. It puts support staff at the center and leads to work that can actually ship.
- **Most fun:** #8 (Time Travel) or #9 (Delight).
- **Safest for a mixed group:** #3 (Our Own Worst Tools). Everyone is an expert user of internal tools.
