# Keep the Saturday Market on Fenwick Square

The Pellbridge town council is considering a proposal to move the Saturday farmers' market from Fenwick Square to the car park behind the new leisure centre. The case for the move is tidy: the car park has more space, easier loading for stallholders, and no need to close three streets every weekend. The case against it is harder to put in a spreadsheet, but I think it is stronger.

The market's value to Pellbridge was never mainly about vegetables. Most residents buy most of their food at the two supermarkets on the ring road, and will continue to. What the market does is bring about two thousand people into the town centre on a Saturday morning, and those people do not only buy leeks. The traders' association's own count last summer found that footfall on the three streets around the square was roughly double on market mornings compared with other Saturdays, and the cafés and the bookshop on Fenwick Street say their Saturday takings depend on it.

Move the market to the leisure centre and those two thousand people will still come, park, shop and leave. They will simply never walk past the high street. The market will survive; the town centre will lose its best morning of the week.

The proposal's supporters point to the cost of closing the streets, about £18,000 a year in traffic management, and to complaints from residents about noise and early-morning setup. Both are real. But £18,000 is modest next to the rates the town-centre businesses pay, and the noise complaints come mostly from a handful of flats directly above the square, whose residents could reasonably be offered a later setup time.

There is a better compromise available. Keep the market on the square, but move the loading bay to Chapel Lane so vans no longer idle outside residential windows, and push the start of setup from 6:00 to 7:00. Ask the traders' association to contribute to the traffic management costs in exchange for a longer lease. Review the arrangement in two years with actual footfall and business data rather than competing anecdotes.

A town centre is not a single business that can be optimised for convenience. It is a set of places that depend on each other's customers. The market on Fenwick Square is one of the few things that still makes those customers walk from one place to the next. The council should keep it where it is.
