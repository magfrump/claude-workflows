# Clearpath: Seed Extension Memo

## Overview

Clearpath keeps mid-market employers (200-2,000 employees) compliant with state and local employment law. It tracks every jurisdiction where a company has employees, flags upcoming changes to leave, pay-transparency and scheduling rules, and generates the policy updates and notices HR needs to publish. Customers are typically HR teams of two to eight people who used to track this by reading law-firm newsletters.

We have 120 customers on annual contracts averaging $24,000 a year ($2,000 per month).

## Unit economics today

**Contribution margin.** After hosting, the legal-content subscriptions we license, card and ACH fees, and the support and onboarding team (all of which we treat as variable and allocate per customer), each customer contributes 75% of revenue, or $1,500 per month. The legal-content licenses are priced per customer, so this margin should hold as we grow. We do not project margin expansion.

**Retention.** Annual logo churn is 9% and net revenue retention is 103%, measured across two full renewal cycles. Compliance is a system of record once it is set up, and the main churn reason is acquisition of the customer by a larger company.

**CAC.** Clearpath is sales-led. Our fully loaded CAC is $24,000, covering SDR and AE salaries, commission, marketing programs, and the sales engineer who runs demos. Inbound customers cost about $19,000 and outbound about $27,000, and the mix has been roughly 40/60 for a year. We have modelled CAC flat because the mix is stable and we have more than 30,000 target companies we have not yet contacted.

**LTV.** $1,500 × 12 = $18,000 annual contribution, divided by 9% annual churn, gives an LTV of $200,000 and an LTV/CAC of 8.3x. We treat this as an upper bound, since it assumes today's churn holds for more than a decade.

**Payback.** $24,000 / $1,500 = 16 months. That is within the 18-24 month range generally considered acceptable for sales-led mid-market SaaS, and our low churn means almost every customer who is signed pays back.

## Current financial position

- Cash on hand: $0.6M
- Fixed operating costs (engineering, legal content team, G&A, excluding sales and marketing): $250,000 per month
- Contribution from the existing 120 customers: $180,000 per month
- Net burn before any new acquisition: $70,000 per month

We are currently signing about 8 customers a month.

## The plan

The sales team we hired last year is now ramped. With three more AEs and two SDRs, we can move to 20 new customers a month starting next month and hold that pace for the next 18 months, which takes us to 480 customers.

## Sizing the raise

We are raising a $3.0M seed extension, sized as follows:

1. **Fixed burn gap.** $70,000 a month for 18 months = $1.26M.
2. **Initial acquisition.** The first three months of acquisition at 20 customers a month (60 customers × $24,000 = $1.44M). Because each customer pays back its CAC in 16 months, from the fourth month onward acquisition is funded by contribution from the customers we have already signed, so it does not need to come from the raise.
3. **Buffer.** $0.30M.

Total: $3.0M. With the existing $0.6M, this gives 18 months of runway, and the model reaches cash-flow breakeven in month 12 as new cohorts come online.

## Why this is a good use of capital

Our payback and LTV/CAC are both better than median for mid-market SaaS, and demand is driven by regulation that only gets more complicated. Five states passed new pay-transparency laws last year alone. We would rather put capital into a proven sales motion than into new products. The engineering team stays flat at nine people through the plan, focused on jurisdiction coverage and the HRIS integrations customers ask for most.

## Risks we are watching

- A large HRIS vendor could bundle basic compliance tracking. Our response is depth: we cover 160 jurisdictions at the city and county level, while bundled tools cover states.
- Sales hiring could slip. The plan assumes the new AEs ramp in three months, which matches the last two hires.
- Legal-content licensing costs could rise at renewal. Our current contract runs two more years.
