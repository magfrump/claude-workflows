# Decision brief: hosting for the Brightmoor remote build cache

**Decision.** The Brightmoor developer-productivity team is choosing where to
host the remote build cache for our monorepo's CI. Options: (a) a managed
cache service from our CI vendor, (b) self-hosted cache nodes on our existing
Kubernetes cluster, or (c) an object-storage bucket behind the open-source
cache proxy.

**Who is deciding.** The developer-productivity team (four engineers), who will
run whichever option we choose. We asked the three product teams that push the
most CI jobs what they need; all three said hit rate and upload speed, and none
has a stake in the hosting itself.

**Context.** The cache holds build outputs keyed by content hash. Every entry
can be regenerated by rebuilding, so losing the cache costs CI time, not data.
Clients talk to it over the standard remote-cache protocol, which all three
options speak, so switching means changing one URL in the shared CI config.
We tried this in January by pointing a branch at a trial bucket for a week:
the switch took under an hour and the cache warmed up again within a day. The
cache is a supporting service, and we expect our needs to stay stable for the
next two years or so. We will revisit if the monorepo's size doubles.

**Criteria.**

1. Cache hit rate and p95 fetch latency, measured over a two-week trial of
   each option on a copy of real CI traffic.
2. Monthly cost at our current 9 TB and at a projected 18 TB.
3. On-call load: the team already runs the Kubernetes cluster, so (b) adds
   pages to an existing rota, while (a) and (c) add a vendor dependency.

**How we will record it.** A short decision record with the trial numbers,
readable by any engineer, plus the dashboards the team already uses for CI.

**Constraints.** The current cache disk runs out in about ten weeks. Budget
is approved up to the cost of option (b).
