# Trussline: Series A Memo

## What Trussline does

Trussline is project-management software for mid-size general contractors. It
connects the estimate, the schedule and the field: when a superintendent logs a
change on site, Trussline shows the cost and schedule impact the same day, and
flags change orders that have not been billed to the owner. Across our 71
customers, unbilled change-order value found in the first 90 days averaged 1.8%
of contract value. We are raising $18M.

## Market

We sell to US general contractors with annual revenue between $10 million and
$250 million. Below that band, contractors run projects from a spreadsheet and
the owner's memory; above it, they buy enterprise suites with dedicated
implementation teams. Census and industry-association data put about 45,000
general contractors in the band.

Our serviceable market is the 60% of those contractors who already pay for some
project-management or estimating software, because they have budget and a
workflow to replace; contractors with no software at all have a much longer
sales cycle. That gives 27,000 contractors. At our average contract value of
$28,000, the serviceable market is $756 million. We present that figure as
both our addressable and serviceable market, because we have no plans to sell
outside the band during the period this plan covers.

## Why now

Two legacy estimating suites, Keystone Estimator and Brickwell, together hold
most of the installed base in our band. Both announced in 2025 that they will
end support for their on-premise versions by the end of 2026, and both are
moving customers to cloud subscriptions at roughly twice the old maintenance
price. For the first time in fifteen years, most mid-size contractors must
choose new software on a fixed deadline, and the incumbent's migration offer is
more expensive than switching.

## Go-to-market

Our beachhead is Colorado and Utah, where our founders ran operations at a
regional contractor and where 58 of our 71 customers are. We sell through a
direct field team supported by referrals from the regional chapters of the
general contractors' association. From the beachhead we expand along the
Mountain West and then into Texas and the Southeast.

## Benchmarks and trajectory

The best guide to what is achievable is the track record of the companies that
have already built construction software businesses. We looked at the three
names most often mentioned by the contractors we sell to.

| Company | Founded | ARR in year 5 |
|---|---|---|
| Buildgrid | 2012 | $110M |
| Sitework | 2016 | $140M |
| Punchlist Pro | 2017 | $104M |

All three passed $100 million in ARR within five years of launch. Sitework's
fastest growth came in 2020 and 2021, when residential and warehouse
construction surged and contractors adopted remote-collaboration tools during
site-access restrictions.

Against that benchmark, our plan is deliberately conservative. We project $38
million in ARR at the end of year five, about 1,350 contractors, which is 5% of
our serviceable market and about a third of the average year-five ARR of the
three companies above. Even if we execute at half the pace of the category
leaders, we reach our target.

## Sales capacity

At our current contract value, $38 million in ARR requires about 1,350
customers. Our field reps close an average of 3.5 contractors a month after a
six-month ramp, so the plan requires 22 quota-carrying reps by the start of
year four. The Series A funds the first twelve; the remainder is funded from
revenue and a later round.

## Use of funds

The $18M round funds twelve field reps, an integrations team for the three most
common accounting systems in our band, and 30 months of runway.
