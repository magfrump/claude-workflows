# Larderly — Growth Plan and Series A Narrative

## Summary

Larderly is a weekly meal-planning app for households. A family tells us who
eats what, what is in the cupboard and how much time they have on weeknights,
and Larderly produces a week of dinners and a single consolidated shopping list
that sends straight to a grocery basket. We have 410,000 monthly active
households, 62,000 of them on the $7.99 monthly subscription, and we are raising
$14M to reach one million paying households over the next three years.

## Why households stay

Larderly gets better the longer a household uses it. Every rating, skipped
recipe and swapped ingredient refines the household's taste profile, and after
about eight weeks the plan needs almost no editing. That profile, along with the
household's saved recipes, pantry inventory and dietary rules for each family
member, lives in Larderly and has no export format that another planner could
read. Subscribers who have been with us for six months churn at under 3% a
month, against roughly 11% in the first month.

We describe our advantage as a personalisation moat built on household-level
data that a new entrant starts without. We are careful not to call it a network
effect: one household's data does not make the product better for another.

## How we grow

Growth has been almost entirely organic, and we intend to keep it that way.

**App Store.** About 71% of new installs come from Apple App Store search and
editorial placement. We rank first for "meal planner" and in the top three for
"meal plan", "dinner ideas" and "grocery list". We were featured in the App
Store's "Get Organised for the New Year" collection two Januaries running, and
each feature produced a month of installs roughly four times our baseline. Our
App Store optimisation work — screenshots, keyword fields, review prompts — is
the single most productive thing our growth team does.

**Short-form video.** Another 17% of installs trace to organic short-form video,
mostly on TikTok, where our "what we ate this week for $80" series regularly
reaches seven-figure view counts. We post five times a week and collaborate with
around forty food creators, none of them paid.

**Everything else.** Word of mouth, press and our web recipe pages make up the
remaining 12%.

Our plan assumes these channels keep producing installs at the same cost per
subscriber they do today. We have modelled blended acquisition cost as flat at
$6 per paying household across the three-year plan, because our spend on these
channels is mostly team time rather than media, and team time does not rise in
proportion to installs. We do not plan to start paid acquisition; our view is
that a company growing this efficiently on owned channels should not introduce
an expensive one.

The round funds growth primarily through the same playbook: a larger App Store
optimisation and lifecycle team, more video production, and localised store
listings in four new English-speaking markets, where we expect our keyword
rankings to transfer.

## Competition

Recipe sites and cooking magazines have meal-plan features, but they are built
around content and advertising rather than around the household's week. Grocery
retailers offer "shop by recipe", but only for their own store, and families
rarely use a single grocer. The large recipe platforms could build planning
tools, and some have tried; their incentive is time on site, whereas ours is
getting dinner on the table in as little time as possible. We think the
household profile makes our plan measurably better for any family that has used
it for two months, and a competitor would need the same months of use to catch
up with each individual household.

## Milestones

- 1,000,000 paying households by end of year three.
- Grocery basket integration with three more national chains in year one.
- Launch a two-adult "household sync" feature so partners share one plan.
- Reach break-even on subscription revenue in month 30.
