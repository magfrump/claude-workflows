# Checkwise — Strategy Brief for the Board

## What we do

Checkwise replaces paper inspection checklists for small property-maintenance
and facilities contractors — the five-to-fifty-person firms that inspect fire
doors, HVAC units, rooftop equipment and rental turnovers for landlords and
building managers. A supervisor builds a checklist template in our web editor;
technicians complete it on a phone, with photos and signatures; the customer
receives a branded PDF report the same afternoon. We charge $29 per technician
per month, billed monthly, and today serve 1,850 contractor firms with about
14,000 technician seats.

## Traction

We have grown seats 9% month over month for the past year, almost entirely
through a free tier that allows one technician and ten reports a month.
Contractors try Checkwise on a single job, show the report to their customer,
and upgrade when the customer asks for the same format on every job. Net revenue
retention is 118%, driven by firms adding technicians.

## Our moat: switching costs

Our core defensibility is high switching costs. Once a contractor has moved its
inspections onto Checkwise, leaving becomes very painful:

- **Their templates live with us.** A typical firm has built between ten and
  thirty checklist templates. Rebuilding them elsewhere is a chore nobody wants.
- **Their history lives with us.** Every past inspection, photo and signed report
  is stored in Checkwise. We make this easy to take with you — any report or the
  full archive can be downloaded as PDF or CSV in one click from the settings
  page, because contractors told us that was a condition of trusting a new vendor.
- **Their technicians know the app.** Training a technician takes about ten
  minutes, and our onboarding video is the most-watched page in our help centre.
- **Their customers expect our reports.** Landlords have grown used to the
  Checkwise PDF layout.

We keep contracts month-to-month and have no minimum seat commitment. We see
this as a strength: customers stay because they want to, and our retention
numbers reflect genuine loyalty rather than lock-in. Logo churn averages 2.1%
monthly.

Over the next eighteen months, switching costs will deepen further. Our
integrations module — two-way sync with the three leading field-service job
management systems and with landlords' maintenance-ticket portals — is planned
for version 3, which we expect to ship in the second half of next year. Once
inspections are wired into the job and billing system on one side and the
landlord's ticketing on the other, a firm leaving Checkwise would have to
re-plumb both.

## Distribution

The free tier is our main acquisition engine and accounts for roughly 80% of new
paying firms. The remainder come from a two-person inside-sales team that works
larger firms (twenty or more technicians), usually after a supervisor at that firm
has already used the free tier. At $29 a seat, a twenty-technician firm is worth
about $7,000 a year, which comfortably supports a short, demo-led inside-sales
motion and nothing heavier. We also get steady referrals from landlords who ask
their other contractors to send reports "like the Checkwise ones".

## Network effect

We do not claim a network effect. Landlord referrals are a helpful growth loop,
but landlords do not use Checkwise directly and gain nothing from more
contractors joining.

## Competitive landscape

Three kinds of competitor exist. Generic form builders are cheaper but lack
offline mode, photo annotation and the branded report. Field-service management
suites include inspection forms, but they cost five to ten times as much per seat
and take months to roll out, so they sell to firms much larger than ours. A few
vertical inspection apps target single trades such as fire safety. If a
field-service suite launched a lightweight, low-priced inspection product aimed
at small contractors, it would be our most serious threat; we think the suites'
pricing and sales models make that unlikely within two years, and we plan to be
integrated with all three of them by then, which would make us a partner rather
than a target.

## What we need from the board

Approval to hire four engineers for the integrations module, and support for
raising prices to $35 per seat for new customers next quarter.
