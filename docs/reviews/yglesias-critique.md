# Yglesias Critique: SWR-Bench Multi-Dimensional Judge & Audit System (v2.0)

**Reviewed:** 2026-08-03 · **Draft:** /workspace/docs/gemini-SWRench.md (AI-generated, Gemini)
**Factual foundation:** /workspace/docs/reviews/fact-check-report.md (10 claims: 6 accurate, 3 mostly accurate, 1 inaccurate)

## The Goal vs. the Mechanism

The goal is right and worth taking at face value: the original SWR-Bench judge counts any predicted finding that doesn't hit reviewer-derived ground truth as a false positive, and your own measurements show this is the binding constraint — 4 TP out of 81 predicted points on the n=7 run, with clean PRs collecting 12 "critical-sounding" findings each. If some of those findings are actually good, the benchmark is punishing the tool for being right. Fixing that is a legitimate measurement problem.

The mechanism does not fix it. It relabels it.

The core move of the spec is the **VU (Valid Unattested, +0.8)** category: a finding that matches no ground truth gets nearly full credit if the judge's own Stage 1 fact-check says it's true. But "is this unmatched finding actually valid?" is precisely the question the whole exercise exists to answer — the adapter doc says so in as many words: *"Whether ambers on decade-old astropy PRs are 'false' positives is exactly what the judge run will quantify"* (swrbench-adapter-2026-07-31.md:80-81, and fact-check C3). The spec resolves this open empirical question by construction: it builds a scorer that assumes unmatched findings are frequently valid, then awards them points on the sole authority of an unvalidated LLM fact-check. There is no ground truth behind VU. Nothing anchors it. A judge that is slightly credulous about plausible-sounding findings converts the measured firehose (~11.6 findings/PR) directly into score, and no artifact in the system can detect that it happened.

Compare what the original judge had going for it: the published benchmark validated LLM-as-judge specifically on *hit-matching*, at 89–95% agreement with humans — and the same paper found LLM *severity* scoring unreliable. Your own tracker drew the correct lesson: "keep the valid/invalid verdict human and let the LLM do only matching" (tracker, Thread 4/Phase 2). This spec inverts that. It demotes matching — the one task the judge is validated on — to Stage 4, and makes validity (Stage 1) and severity (Stage 3) — the tasks the evidence says LLM judges do badly — the load-bearing gates. The mechanism concentrates trust exactly where trust is least earned.

Then there's the ordering bug the fact-check flagged (C7): because severity is checked *before* ground-truth attestation, a finding that a human reviewer actually raised — the gold standard the whole benchmark is built on — scores **−0.1** if the judge deems it low-severity. The stated goal is "do not penalize valid findings simply because historical reviewers omitted them"; the mechanism penalizes valid findings that historical reviewers *included*. Your own values say attested findings are the most trustworthy signal in the dataset; the pipeline routes a chunk of them into the noise bucket.

Finally, WUS has no recall term. The score is mean utility per prediction, so a tool that emits one safe finding per PR can beat a tool that finds every ground-truth issue plus modest noise, and a tool that says nothing at all is undefined rather than zero. The original F1 at least punished silence. If precision-vs-noise is your concern, fine — but then say the benchmark no longer measures whether the tool finds the bugs humans cared about, because under WUS it doesn't.

## The Boring Lever

The original judge already grades every potential false positive on a 1–10 inherent-severity scale (evaluation_struct.py:40, 223 — fact-check C2/C4). That field is sitting in every existing judgment file, unused.

The boring lever: **re-analyze the stock judge's output instead of replacing the judge.** Bucket unmatched findings by their existing severity grade, report precision with and without the low-severity band, and — if you want a validity signal — human-adjudicate the unmatched findings on your 30-PR sample using the persistent label store the tracker already specified (Phase 2: ~120 unique labels after dedup, amortized forever). That gets you:

- the TP/plausible-VU/NB discrimination this spec wants, at roughly zero new judge code;
- both old and new numbers computed from the *same* run, so comparability with the pinned Claude-CLI judge and the published tables survives;
- actual ground truth for the "are the ambers valid?" question, instead of an LLM's opinion of itself.

Impact estimate: the existing severity field plus one afternoon of human labeling on 30 PRs delivers most of the discrimination the 4-stage pipeline promises, and it delivers the one thing the pipeline structurally cannot — an answer to the validity question that isn't generated by the system under audit. The spec's version is a scoreboard redesign; the lever is a measurement.

## Follow the Money

Trace where the money (judge calls, engineering hours, human attention) actually goes:

- **Judge compute:** roughly fine. The §5 prompt does all four checks in one structured call, so per-finding judge cost is comparable to today's. At 30 PRs × ~12 findings that's ~360 calls — cents to dollars. Not the problem.
- **Engineering time (the scarce resource — one solo dev):** the §4 static-analysis integration (libCST + Ruff + "Mypy in AST mode") is the biggest line item, and it buys almost nothing. Per fact-check C8, the named toolchain cannot parse Python 2 at all and one of its three components doesn't exist — so the build-out fails on the exact legacy slice (64 PRs from 2011–2012) that §4 invokes as its motivation. Meanwhile §4.3 — forcing the judge to quote exact code and line numbers, verified mechanically — is nearly free, needs no parsers, and is the part with real precision evidence behind it (the tracker's quote-grounding recommendation). The spec spends the dollar on the machinery and the dime on the mechanism that works.
- **Human audit attention:** §6's audit reports are genuinely good — auditability is the right instinct. But the 60-second spot-check budget times 360 findings is 6 hours per 30-PR run, and the reports can only surface disagreements a human happens to look at. Since VU has no ground truth, audit attention is the *only* check on the most generous category — meaning the scarcest resource in the whole system is assigned the load-bearing validation role, informally, with no sampling plan.

Net: most of the new spend goes to a toolchain that can't run on its target and a pipeline that shifts trust onto unvalidated judge sub-tasks. The fraction reaching the actual outcome — knowing whether unmatched findings are valid — is close to zero, because that question is answered by assumption, not by any resource in the budget.

## Factual Foundation

Four fact-check findings are load-bearing for mechanism viability:

- **C8 (Inaccurate):** §4's toolchain — libCST is Python-3-only, Ruff is Python-3-only, "Mypy in AST mode" is not a real mode, and mypy dropped Python 2 in 2022. The static-verification protocol as specified cannot process the 64 Python-2-era instances it exists to handle. This isn't a nitpick; §4 is one of the spec's three headline features.
- **C3 (Mostly accurate):** the motivating claim — spot-checks revealed penalized "high-quality, valid findings" — presents an open question as a settled finding. The spec's entire scoring redesign inherits this assumption; see Goal vs. Mechanism.
- **C2/C4 (Mostly accurate):** the spec strawmans the original as naive "explicit request" matching and "single-dimensional." The original matches semantically and already severity-grades FPs 1–10. This matters because the strawman hides the boring lever: the discrimination the spec wants partially exists in the current output.
- **C7 flag:** severity-before-attestation routes human-attested low-severity findings to −0.1, contradicting the spec's own motivation. (The §3 diagram is also garbled in the Stage 3/4 region — cosmetic, but it's the region with the design bug, which is not a great look for a spec whose acceptance criteria include human auditability.)

## The Scale Test

Scale from the 7–30 PR spot-check to the full 1,000-instance dataset, multiple ACR tools, repeated runs:

1. **The VU anchor problem compounds.** With no ground truth behind VU, judge drift (model updates, prompt tweaks, provider swaps — you already had to swap Gemini→Claude-CLI once because of a key outage) shifts scores with no way to distinguish "the tools got better" from "the judge got softer." At 30 PRs you can eyeball it; at 1,000 × N tools × repeated runs, the only detector is the human audit, and 1,000 PRs × ~12 findings × 60 seconds is **200 hours of spot-checking per run**. The audit mechanism that makes the system trustworthy at demo scale does not exist at benchmark scale, and the spec has no sampling plan to replace it.
2. **The audit path collides.** `audit_reports/{repo_owner}_{repo_name}/pr_{pr_number}.md` has no run ID, tool ID, or model ID. The second tool you evaluate silently overwrites the first tool's audit trail. At one tool, one run, it works; at the stated purpose of the benchmark (comparing ACR tools over time), the artifact layer destroys its own history.
3. **WUS's missing recall term becomes the optimization target.** One tool at 30 PRs, nobody games anything. Multiple tools compared on a leaderboard number, and the winning strategy is emitting few ultra-safe findings — the metric actively selects against the recall that made the benchmark informative.

None of these is a compute constraint; they're feedback-loop failures that only appear when the system is used for its stated purpose at its stated size.

## The Org Chart

Who runs this? One solo developer plus LLM agents, running locally, currently key-blocked from the original Gemini judge and operating through a pinned Claude-CLI shim that deliberately preserved *stock* prompts, parsers, and metrics — a decision made specifically so results stay "internally comparable across our arms as long as this judge stays pinned" (adapter doc). The first three lines of the job description for whoever maintains v2.0:

1. Keep a 4-stage judge pipeline, its JSON schema, and its parser in sync across judge-model swaps you don't control (you've already been forced into one).
2. Maintain a three-tool static-analysis integration spanning Python 2 and 3 — with a toolchain that cannot parse Python 2.
3. Manually spot-check audit reports at a rate that scales linearly with dataset size, tools, and runs.

Line 2 is impossible as written and line 3 doesn't fit in a solo developer's calendar. The track record cuts the same way: the one integration this repo has actually shipped succeeded *because* it changed the minimum possible surface (transport only). This spec proposes changing everything — taxonomy, metrics, prompts, parsers, artifacts — at once, with no migration plan and no judge-validation step (the original's 89–95% human-agreement measurement has no v2 counterpart anywhere in the spec). That's still at the wish stage.

## Adoption Survival

A benchmark survives its adoption cycle by producing numbers people keep trusting. Today's setup has two credibility anchors: the published SWR-Bench tables (external comparability) and the pinned-judge discipline (internal comparability). v2.0 as specified severs both — WUS is comparable to nothing, and the unvalidated VU category gives any skeptic (including future-you, six months from now, staring at a suspiciously improved score) a one-sentence dismissal: *"the judge that was told not to penalize the tool stopped penalizing the tool."* That creates opponents, not defenders. It's Claude judging Claude-authored reviews, now with a rubric that pre-authorizes generosity.

The popular version — 80% of the benefit, 20% of the cost, and it *creates* defenders: keep the stock judge and metrics untouched; add a post-hoc analysis layer that (a) re-buckets unmatched findings using the existing 1–10 severity field, (b) reports WUS-style weighted views *alongside* stock P/R/F1, never instead of them, and (c) human-adjudicates the unmatched findings on the 30-PR sample to calibrate how often "plausible VU" is actually valid. Every run then yields both the old trustworthy number and the new charitable number, and the gap between them is itself the finding you wanted.

## The Cost Disease Check

The relevant cost disease here is judge-capability inflation: the belief that pouring a smarter judge, more stages, and more prompt structure into the evaluation produces proportionally better measurement. The spec assumes it; the evidence in this very repo points the other way — more pipeline means more unvalidated sub-judgments (validity, scope, severity) stacked in sequence, each an unmeasured error rate, with the final label as reliable as the *weakest* stage. More judge input does not produce more measurement output unless something exogenous (human labels, executable tests, the head/base gate from Thread 7) anchors it, and the spec contains no exogenous anchor at all.

## Overall Assessment

The goal is sound and the diagnosis is half right: the original benchmark really does score some presumably-useful findings as false positives, and your n=7 numbers show the penalty is large. But the mechanism is wish-fulfillment on the load-bearing point — it converts "are these findings valid?" from an empirical question into a scoring assumption, enforced by an unvalidated LLM judging tasks (validity, severity) that the benchmark's own literature says LLM judges do unreliably, while demoting the one task (matching) they're validated on. Add the impossible §4 toolchain, the attested-findings-scored-negative ordering bug, the missing recall term, and the collision-prone audit paths, and this spec is not implementable as written by the org that has to implement it.

**Single most important revision:** make v2.0 an *analysis layer over* the stock judge rather than a replacement — stock metrics always reported, WUS reported beside them, and the VU category calibrated against human adjudication of unmatched findings on the existing 30-PR sample before its +0.8 weight is trusted anywhere. That one change preserves comparability, adds the exogenous anchor, and shrinks the engineering surface to something a solo developer can actually maintain. The taxonomy is a genuinely useful vocabulary; earn it numbers worth attaching to it.

**Load-bearing objection:** VU (+0.8) is awarded solely by an unvalidated LLM fact-check with no ground truth and no calibration step, so any score improvement under v2.0 is indistinguishable from judge leniency — which defeats the entire purpose of changing the metric, since you still won't know whether the penalized findings were valid.

Ranked beneath it:
1. §4's static-verification toolchain cannot parse the Python 2-era slice it exists for, and one named component doesn't exist (fact-check C8) — a headline feature that fails at spec time.
2. Severity-before-attestation routes human-attested low-severity findings to −0.1, penalizing the benchmark's own gold standard and contradicting the spec's stated goal.
3. WUS has no recall term and audit paths have no run/tool ID, so at multi-tool scale the metric rewards silence and the audit trail overwrites itself.
