## Verdict for C1: "The US has the best healthcare in the world."

**Verdict:** Inaccurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The best-known comparison of national health systems, the Commonwealth Fund's *Mirror, Mirror 2024*, ranks the US last overall among 10 high-income countries. Australia, the Netherlands and the UK rank highest. The US also ranks last on health outcomes, including life expectancy and avoidable deaths, and last on affordability. Its one strong result is second place in the "care process" domain. I found no major cross-national ranking that puts the US first overall. Whether one system is "best" depends partly on how you weigh things, but the claim as stated contradicts the main ranking that measures it. Confidence is Medium: there is one primary source, read only in excerpts, and several outlets reporting the same finding. A full reading of the report plus a second independent ranking (for example OECD outcome indicators) could raise it to High.

**Sources:** Commonwealth Fund, *Mirror, Mirror 2024* (Sept 2024); news coverage of that report in AJMC, Fortune and Becker's (2024)
**Citation:** https://commonwealthfund.org/press-release/2024/new-international-study-us-health-system-fails-many-americans-ranks-lowest

## Verdict for C2: "Our survival rates for many cancers lead all other nations"

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** inferred
**Scrutiny:** abstract

CONCORD-3 (Allemani et al., *Lancet* 2018; 71 countries, patients diagnosed 2000–14) places the US among the countries with the highest survival for most cancers. It shares that group with Canada, Australia, New Zealand and the Nordic countries. On breast cancer the US has the top figure, 90.2%, but Australia is close behind at 89.5%. For colon cancer the top figure in the excerpts I found was South Korea's (about 71.8%), not the US. The evidence supports "among the highest for many cancers." It does not support the US leading every other nation on many cancers. The reasoning: specific figures from the paper (breast cancer: US first by a small margin; colon cancer: Korea first) plus its summary statement that the US is in a group of top performers. Confidence is capped at Medium because I couldn't open the paper's country-by-cancer tables. Reading them would settle how many of the 18 cancers the US actually ranks first on.

**Sources:** Allemani et al., "Global surveillance of trends in cancer survival 2000–14 (CONCORD-3)," *The Lancet* (2018), PubMed 29395269; OECD material on breast cancer survival (US and Cyprus about 9 in 10)
**Citation:** "5-year survival is now 89·5% in Australia and 90·2% in the USA"

## Verdict for C3: "patients from around the globe travel to American hospitals for cutting-edge treatments"

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** inferred
**Scrutiny:** abstract

Several independent secondary sources agree that a substantial number of patients travel to the US for care. Deloitte estimated about 400,000 non-US residents in 2008. Cleveland Clinic alone saw 9,150 international patients in 2019, according to Advisory Board. A USITC briefing reports that health-related travel into the US has grown. The national totals vary a lot by source (about 300,000 to 1.4 million a year) and the higher figures come from industry groups, but the draft doesn't give a number. The "cutting-edge treatments" part is less well documented. One low-quality source lists cosmetic surgery, dentistry and orthopedics as the main procedures. This claim reaches Medium through agreeing secondary sources; the USITC trade data, read in full, would supply a primary source.

**Sources:** Deloitte Center for Health Solutions (2008 estimate); Advisory Board Daily Briefing (2022); USITC Executive Briefing on Trade, health-related travel; Statista (2020 source markets)
**Citation:** "400,000 non-U.S. residents sought medical care in the United States in 2008"

## Claims Requiring Author Attention

- **C1:** The major international ranking (Commonwealth Fund 2024) puts the US last of 10 high-income countries overall, not first. No ranking found puts the US first overall.
- **C2:** CONCORD-3 shows the US is among the top performers for many cancers. It does not show the US ahead of every other nation on many cancers: breast cancer is close to a tie with Australia, and South Korea leads on colon cancer.
