# Ferrule: Series A Market Narrative

## What we do

Ferrule automates the slowest part of answering a public-records request:
finding and redacting exempt information before documents are released. A
records officer uploads the responsive emails, PDFs and spreadsheets, and
Ferrule proposes redactions for personal data, law-enforcement material and
other statutory exemptions, each tagged with the exemption that justifies it.
The officer reviews, accepts or overrides, and exports a release package with a
redaction log. Our customers today are 64 counties and cities across five
states. Median time to close a request at those customers fell from 19 working
days to 7 in the first year of use.

## Why public bodies buy

Request volumes have risen every year for a decade, but the step change came
from statute. Between 2023 and 2025, eleven states shortened their statutory
response deadlines and added per-day penalties for late responses, and six of
those states now publish each agency's on-time rate. Records officers who used
to absorb the backlog quietly now have a public number attached to their office.
Every one of our 64 customers first contacted us within a year of their state's
deadline change, and three quarters of our current pipeline sits in those eleven
states. We expect the remaining states to follow over the next five years, but
the plan below does not depend on it.

## Serviceable market

We sized the market bottom-up, by type of public body, using the number of
bodies that receive enough requests to justify a dedicated tool and the annual
contract value we see or expect for each type.

| Segment | Bodies | ACV | Annual value |
|---|---|---|---|
| Federal agencies and components | 440 | $120,000 | $52.8M |
| State agencies | 2,900 | $30,000 | $87.0M |
| Counties | 3,031 | $18,000 | $54.6M |
| Municipalities over 10,000 residents | 3,300 | $9,000 | $29.7M |
| School districts over 5,000 students | 13,000 | $6,000 | $78.0M |
| **Total** | | | **$302.1M** |

Federal and state agencies together are $139.8M, or 46% of the serviceable
market, and they carry the highest contract values, because a single federal
component can receive tens of thousands of requests a year. They are the
centre of our growth plan.

## Platform and compliance

Ferrule runs in a single commercial cloud region in the United States. We
completed a SOC 2 Type I audit in 2025 and expect our Type II report in the
second quarter of next year. We plan to begin the FedRAMP Moderate
authorisation process in 2028, once the business can fund a dedicated
compliance team; we have not yet scoped StateRAMP. None of our current
customers has required either.

## Share and trajectory

We plan to reach $15M in ARR by the end of year five, which is 5% of the
$302M serviceable market. Our revenue model assumes the federal and
state-agency segments contribute $8M of that, starting with a first federal
contract in year two and a first state-agency enterprise agreement in year
two, and ramping from there. Counties and cities, where we already sell
successfully, contribute $5M, and school districts contribute $2M from year
three onward.

The county and city numbers are the ones we can defend best: we win about one
in three county evaluations today, our reference customers are active in the
state associations of counties, and the procurement cycle is a single
department head plus a council vote.

## Comparable companies

Two companies have built comparable businesses selling workflow software to
records and clerk offices. Clerkstream, which sells agenda and minutes software
to municipalities, reached $41M in ARR nine years after launch. Docketwise
Public, which sold case-management software to county courts, reached $12M and
was acquired after seven years; its founders cite the length of the court
procurement cycle as the reason growth flattened. We sit between the two: a
faster procurement cycle than courts, but a narrower product than agenda
management.
