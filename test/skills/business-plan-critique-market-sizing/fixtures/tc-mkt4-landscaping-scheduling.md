# Plotwise: Pre-Seed Plan

## The product

Plotwise schedules and dispatches crews for landscaping and grounds-maintenance
companies. An owner enters recurring contracts once (weekly mowing, seasonal
cleanups, irrigation checks), and Plotwise builds each day's routes, pushes
them to crew leads' phones, records completed visits with photos, and generates
invoices at month end. We have 58 paying companies, all in Texas, after nine
months of selling.

## Who buys

There are about 96,000 landscaping and grounds-maintenance businesses in the
United States with at least one employee. Most are very small: an owner and a
truck. We sell to companies with two or more crews, because that is the point
where the owner can no longer hold every route in their head and starts
double-booking and missing visits. Industry payroll data puts roughly 45% of
employer firms at that size, or about 43,200 companies.

Size alone does not make a buyer. In our sales calls, the companies that buy
are the ones already paying for some kind of software, usually a generic
calendar plus a separate invoicing tool, and complaining about the gap between
them. Firms still running on paper and phone calls rarely convert. Using the
ratio from our first 400 sales conversations, we estimate that 40% of
two-plus-crew companies already pay for scheduling or invoicing software. That
gives a serviceable market of 17,280 companies. At our price of $300 a month
($3,600 a year), that is $62.2 million in annual revenue.

We have not tried to build a larger total market figure from the broader
field-service software category, because most of that category serves trades
with very different jobs, such as HVAC and plumbing, where each visit is a
one-off repair rather than a recurring route.

## Why now

Three things make this the moment for Plotwise. Smartphones are now in the
pocket of every crew lead, so software can finally reach the people doing the
work. Small businesses across every industry are moving their operations to the
cloud and are more comfortable than ever paying monthly for software. And a new
generation of owners, who grew up with apps, is taking over family landscaping
businesses and expects better tools than the ones their parents used.
Together, these shifts mean landscaping is ready for modern software in a way it
has not been before.

## Plan for share

Our beachhead is Texas, where we have our first 58 customers, a partnership
with the state landscaping contractors' association, and about 1,500
qualifying companies. We aim to reach 400 Texas companies by the end of year
two, a little over a quarter of the state's serviceable companies. We then
expand into the rest of the Sun Belt, where the season is long enough that
companies run crews year-round. By the end of year five we project 900
companies, which is about 5% of the national serviceable market and $3.2
million in ARR.

Our sales motion is inside sales plus association partnerships. Two sales reps
closed our first 58 customers; at full ramp we expect each rep to close about
15 companies a month, so the plan needs no more than four reps by year three.

## Comparable companies

Three companies have tried to build route-based scheduling for a single
outdoor trade. Poolroute, built for pool-cleaning companies, reached about
3,000 customers in eight years and is profitable. Snowline, built for
snow-removal contractors, reached 600 customers and was folded into a larger
field-service suite in 2022, because the season was too short to keep customers
paying year-round. Yardly, a consumer marketplace for lawn care, spent heavily
on acquisition and closed in 2021. We model ourselves on Poolroute: a recurring
route business with a year-round season in our target states.
