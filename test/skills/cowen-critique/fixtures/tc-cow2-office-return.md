# Bringing Kestrel Back Together: The Case for Four Office Days

*A proposal to the Kestrel Analytics leadership team*

## Summary

Kestrel Analytics has operated on a fully flexible model since 2021: employees choose when, and whether, to come to the office. This memo proposes that from the start of the next financial year we move to a structured model of four office days a week for all staff within commuting distance of our Riverside and Northgate sites. The case rests on something we rarely lead with in these discussions: our own people are asking for it.

## What our employees told us

In March we ran the most thorough engagement survey in the company's history, with a 71 percent response rate across 640 employees. Three findings stand out.

First, 78 percent of respondents agreed or strongly agreed that "in-person collaboration produces better work than remote collaboration." This held across every department, including engineering, where many of us assumed remote preference was strongest.

Second, 69 percent said they "miss the energy of a full office," and 64 percent said they would "value more opportunities for spontaneous conversations with colleagues."

Third, in free-text responses, the single most common theme was isolation. Words like "lonely," "disconnected" and "siloed" appeared in more than a quarter of comments. Several newer employees wrote that they had never met their manager's manager in person.

These are not the results of a leadership team imposing its preferences. They are the voice of the workforce. Our people value being together, and our current policy is failing to give them that.

## The business case

The survey matches what we see in our work. Cross-team projects take longer than they did in 2019; our internal project tracker shows the median cross-team initiative now runs 23 percent past its planned end date, compared with 11 percent before the pandemic. Onboarding is slower: new analysts take on average fourteen weeks to reach full billable utilisation, up from ten. Mentoring, which used to happen by osmosis, now has to be scheduled, and so it mostly does not happen.

There is also a cultural argument. Kestrel built its reputation on a particular way of working: argumentative, fast, collaborative. That culture was transmitted in rooms. Employees who joined after 2021 have learned a different, more transactional version of Kestrel, and our client feedback scores for "creative problem solving" have drifted down accordingly.

## Why four days, and why now

We considered three days, which is the most common hybrid arrangement in our sector. We rejected it because a three-day policy tends to fragment: teams come in on different days and the office remains half-empty. Four days gives enough overlap that most teams will be together most of the time.

The timing is also right because the facilities are ready. The new Riverside office, opened last spring with 420 desks, two floors of collaboration space and a proper kitchen, is currently running at around 14 percent average desk occupancy under the flexible policy. That spare capacity means we can bring everyone back without leasing additional space, and without the crowding that made the old building unpopular. Northgate has similar headroom.

## Addressing concerns

We expect some pushback, particularly from employees with caring responsibilities or long commutes. The proposal includes:

- an exceptions process, reviewed by HR rather than line managers, for caring and health needs;
- a commuting allowance for staff living more than 25 miles from their site;
- core hours of 10:00 to 15:00, so that people can travel outside peak times.

We also recognise that a small number of employees may choose to leave. Our judgement is that this is an acceptable cost given how clearly the majority has expressed its wishes.

## Recommendation

Approve a four-day office policy effective from 1 April, with a communications plan that leads with the survey results. We are not asking our people to do something they do not want. We are finally giving them what they have told us they want.
