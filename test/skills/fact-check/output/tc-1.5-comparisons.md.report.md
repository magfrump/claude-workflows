## Verdict for C1: "Most OECD countries provide universal pre-K"

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** inferred
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The general direction holds. OECD's *Education at a Glance 2024* says almost every member offers at least one free year of early childhood education before primary school. Eurydice reports that only three EU states (Ireland, Italy, Malta) lack a formal legal entitlement or compulsory ECEC, and of those, Malta isn't an OECD member.

The phrase "universal pre-K" hides a lot of variation, though:
- OECD counts **17 member countries** where children still go at least a year without access to free care (the "childcare gap").
- The age at which a place is guaranteed ranges from infancy to 4–6.
- Some entitlements are part-time.
- By OECD's own measure the US also provides free pre-primary education, through kindergarten at age 5, which leaves a 5-year gap before it. So the claim is only clearly true if "pre-K" means provision for 3- and 4-year-olds.

How I reached the verdict: OECD (almost all members provide free pre-primary for at least the final year) plus Eurydice (most European systems guarantee a place from around age 3 or earlier) supports "most provide guaranteed early education." No source gives an exact count of OECD countries with universal provision at ages 3–4. Two independent primary sources were found, but both only as excerpts, so confidence is capped at Medium. A full read of EAG 2025/2026 Table B2 and the Eurydice *Key Data 2025* entitlement map would settle the count.

**Sources:** OECD, *Education at a Glance 2024*, Chapter B1; OECD, *Education at a Glance 2024* US country note; Eurydice, *Structural Indicators / Key Data on ECEC* (2023, 2025)
**Citation:** "almost all OECD countries provide at least one year of free early childhood education before primary school" — [OECD EAG 2024, B1](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/education-at-a-glance-2024_c00cad36-en/full-report/how-does-participation-in-early-childhood-education-and-care-differ-among-countries_d349c087.html)

## Verdict for C2: "making the United States an outlier among wealthy nations"

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

OECD country notes consistently put US enrolment well below the OECD average:
- **EAG 2025:** 65% of 3–5-year-olds enrolled in 2022, about 20 percentage points under the average and "virtually unchanged since 2013."
- **EAG 2026:** 66% of children aged 3 to primary-school age in 2024, against an OECD average of 90%.
- **EAG 2023 (2021 data):** 50% of 4-year-olds against 87%, and 30% of 3-year-olds against 72%.

These sources describe the US as one of the lowest, which matches the claim. Two country notes give the figures, but they come from the same OECD series and data, so they aren't independent and the rating stays at Medium.

**Sources:** OECD, *Education at a Glance* US country notes, 2025 and 2026; NCES/IES blog on EAG 2023 (2023)
**Citation:** "only 65% of 3-5 year-olds were enrolled in pre-primary education in the United States" — [OECD EAG 2025, United States](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/education-at-a-glance-2025_1a3543e2-en/united-states_784df67f-en.html)

## Verdict for C3: "The lack of a federal pre-K program"

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The US has no universal federal pre-K program. That's consistent with OECD noting that US early childhood policy is set by states, and with the fact that pre-primary enrolment data are reported state by state. But there is a federal early-education program, **Head Start**. It is means-tested: eligibility is mainly family income at or below 100% of the federal poverty guidelines, plus categories like children in foster care or experiencing homelessness, and up to 10% of places can go to over-income children. Wikipedia, a weak source, gives enrolment at about 820,000 children in 2023. Read literally, "no federal pre-K program" overstates the case. Read as "no universal federal pre-K," it's accurate. The rating rests on one primary source (Head Start guidance) seen only as an excerpt, so Medium.

**Sources:** HHS Office of Head Start, eligibility guidance (headstart.gov); Wikipedia, "Head Start (program)" (secondary)
**Citation:** [Head Start, Poverty Guidelines for Determining Eligibility](https://headstart.gov/ersea/article/poverty-guidelines-determining-eligibility-participation-head-start-programs)

## Verdict for C4: "access depends heavily on which state a family lives in"

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

NIEER's *State of Preschool 2025* yearbook (2024–25 school year) shows wide differences between states:
- **National:** 37% of 4-year-olds enrolled in state-funded preschool.
- **Top end:** 12 states enrolled more than half their 4-year-olds, led by the District of Columbia at 94%.
- **Mid-range examples:** Pennsylvania 26%, North Carolina 21%.
- **No program:** six states had no program meeting NIEER's definition: Idaho, Indiana, Montana, New Hampshire, South Dakota and Wyoming. Indiana's program exists but is excluded because eligibility requires parents to work or study.

This is one primary source, seen in excerpts and backed up by news coverage (The 74, K-12 Dive), so Medium.

**Sources:** NIEER, *State of Preschool 2025* Executive Summary and state press releases (2026); The 74 (2026); K-12 Dive (2026)
**Citation:** [NIEER State of Preschool 2025, Executive Summary](https://nieer.org/yearbook/2025/executive-summary)

## Claims Requiring Author Attention

- **C1** — "Universal pre-K" is vague. Nearly all OECD countries give at least one free year before primary school, but 17 still have a gap of a year or more in free provision, entitlement ages vary widely, and the US also offers free kindergarten at 5. The claim holds most clearly if it specifies provision for 3- and 4-year-olds.
- **C3** — A federal early-education program does exist: Head Start, which is means-tested for low-income families. What the US lacks is a *universal* federal pre-K program.
