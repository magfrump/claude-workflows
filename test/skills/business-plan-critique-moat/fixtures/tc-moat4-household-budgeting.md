# Tallyleaf — Investor Narrative

## The one-line version

Tallyleaf is the budgeting app for people who have tried budgeting apps and
quit. It connects to a user's bank accounts, sorts every transaction into
plain-language envelopes, and sends one short message each evening telling the
user what they can still spend this week. No charts, no dashboards, no
spreadsheets.

## Where we are

- 1.3 million registered users, 520,000 monthly active.
- 88,000 subscribers on the $6.99 monthly plan.
- 12-month subscriber retention of 61%, well above what we see reported for the
  personal-finance category.
- Average of 5.2 sessions per week among subscribers.

Each user runs Tallyleaf on their own. There is no sharing, no social feed, no
household mode and no messaging between users; money is private, and our users
tell us that privacy is a large part of why they trust us. Everything a user sees
is computed from their own accounts and their own envelope rules.

## Our moat: network effects

Tallyleaf has strong network effects, and they are the heart of our long-term
defensibility. Every new user makes Tallyleaf more valuable for every other
user, in three ways.

**Brand network effect.** Each new user is a new advocate. Budgeting is
something people talk about with friends and family, and a Tallyleaf user who
finally sticks to a budget tells an average of 2.4 people about us, by our
survey. The bigger we get, the more people have heard of us, the more
trustworthy we look, and the easier the next user is to win. Roughly 58% of new
sign-ups say they heard about Tallyleaf from someone they know.

**Cost network effect.** Our bank-connection provider charges on a tiered
schedule, and our per-connection price falls at each volume tier. Our cloud
costs per user have also halved in two years. The more users we have, the
cheaper each additional user is to serve, which lets us keep our price below
competitors that have fewer users.

**Engagement network effect.** As more people use Tallyleaf, we learn which
evening messages drive people to stay under budget, and we tune the defaults for
new users. Our message wording and send times have been A/B tested on hundreds
of thousands of users.

Put together, these effects create a flywheel: more users mean more
word-of-mouth, lower costs and better defaults, which bring in more users. We
believe this network effect will make Tallyleaf the default budgeting app for
its generation, in the same way that the leading messaging apps became defaults
once everyone's friends were on them.

## Distribution

Most growth is word of mouth, supplemented by a referral program (one free month
for both sides) and personal-finance creators on video platforms, whom we pay a
flat fee per video. The app stores account for most installs, but in our surveys
the store is where people go after hearing the name elsewhere, not where they
discover us. We spend a modest amount on paid social, mostly for retargeting.

## Switching costs

Users set up envelopes and rules over their first few weeks, and Tallyleaf keeps
their full transaction history, categorised. A user who leaves loses the
categorisation and would have to rebuild their envelopes elsewhere. We do not
overstate this; setup takes an evening.

## Competition

The banks' own apps now include spending insights, and two well-funded
budgeting apps target the same users with more detailed tools. We think the
banks' tools are designed to keep customers inside one bank, whereas most of our
users hold accounts at three or more institutions. The detailed budgeting apps
serve people who enjoy spreadsheets; our users do not. If either competitor
simplified its product to match ours, we believe our network effects would keep
our users loyal.

## The round

We are raising $20M to expand into two new markets, hire a creator partnerships
team, and build a savings-goals product. Our network effects mean each market we
enter should become cheaper to win than the last.
