# Expanding Automated Red-Light Enforcement Citywide

**To:** Harrowgate City Council, Transportation and Public Works Committee
**From:** Office of Street Safety
**Re:** FY2026 capital request for 48 additional red-light camera installations

## Summary

In early 2024 the Office of Street Safety installed automated red-light cameras at twelve signalized intersections. Over the following twelve months, injury crashes at those twelve intersections fell from 214 in calendar year 2023 to 131 in calendar year 2024, a reduction of 39 percent. No other intervention on this scale has delivered comparable results for the city at comparable cost.

We propose extending the program to 48 more intersections, bringing the total to 60, and we project that the expansion will reduce injury crashes at the new sites by the same 39 percent. Based on the 2023 crash count at the 48 candidate intersections (412 injury crashes), that is roughly 160 fewer injury crashes per year once the full network is operating.

## How the pilot sites were chosen

When the Council funded the pilot in 2023, it directed the Office to put the first cameras where they would do the most good. We ranked every signalized intersection in the city by its count of injury crashes in 2023 and installed cameras at the top twelve. These were the intersections residents were writing to us about, and several of them had appeared in local news coverage after serious collisions that year.

Installation was completed in February 2024. The cameras photograph vehicles that enter the intersection after the signal turns red, and a sworn officer reviews every citation before it is mailed. Fines are set at $95, and the program has been revenue-neutral since month five.

## Results

| Measure | 2023 (before) | 2024 (after) | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Injury crashes, 12 pilot intersections | 214 | 131 | -39% |
| Red-light violations per camera per day | — | 7.4 (Feb) → 3.1 (Dec) | -58% |
| Citations issued | — | 16,880 | — |
| Resident complaints about the cameras | — | 41 | — |

The drop in violations over the year tells us that drivers learned. Violations fell steadily from the first month to the last, which is the pattern we would expect if the cameras were changing behavior rather than simply catching the same drivers repeatedly.

Staff also surveyed 600 residents living within a half mile of a pilot intersection. Seventy-one percent said they felt safer crossing at those intersections than they had a year earlier.

## The expansion

The 48 candidate intersections are the next 48 on the same 2023 ranking, positions 13 through 60. Their injury crash counts range from 14 down to 5 per year, against 15 to 27 at the pilot sites in 2023. Applying the pilot's 39 percent reduction to their combined 412 crashes gives the projection of about 160 fewer injury crashes per year.

Capital cost is $58,000 per intersection, or $2.78 million for all 48, which we propose to fund from the Vision Zero capital line. Operating costs, including officer review time and the vendor's processing fee, would be covered by citation revenue as they are today.

## Why now

The Council adopted a goal of cutting traffic injuries in half by 2030. Injury crashes citywide have been roughly flat for five years. The camera pilot is the only program in our portfolio that has produced a measured reduction of this size, and every year we wait is a year of avoidable crashes at intersections we have already identified.

## Timeline

- **Q1:** Council approval; vendor contract amendment.
- **Q2–Q3:** Installation in three phases of 16 intersections each, starting with the highest-ranked.
- **Q4:** First full quarter of operation across all 60 sites; staff report to the committee.

## Recommendation

Approve the FY2026 capital request of $2.78 million to install red-light cameras at the 48 intersections ranked 13 through 60, and direct the Office of Street Safety to report injury crash counts at all 60 sites annually.
