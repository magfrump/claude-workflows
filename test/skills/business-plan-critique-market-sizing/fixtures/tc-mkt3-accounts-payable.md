# Tallyroom: Investor Memo

## Overview

Tallyroom automates accounts payable for mid-market companies. Supplier
invoices arrive by email; Tallyroom reads them, matches each line against the
purchase order and goods receipt in the customer's ERP, routes exceptions to
the right approver, and schedules payment. Finance teams at our 38 customers
process invoices at a median cost of $2.10 each, against the $9 to $12 they
reported before switching. We are raising $12M to scale the company.

## Total addressable market

We sized the market two ways and they land close together.

Bottom-up: there are about 64,000 US companies with between 100 and 999
employees. Companies in that band run a dedicated AP function and process
between 800 and 6,000 supplier invoices a month. Our average contract value
across current customers is $38,000 a year, which gives a total addressable
market of $2.43 billion.

Top-down: Halvorsen Group estimates US spending on accounts-payable automation
by companies in the same employee band at between $2.1 billion and $2.6 billion
in 2025. Halvorsen's category covers invoice capture, matching and payment
scheduling, which is our product scope; it excludes expense management and
corporate cards, which we do not sell.

The two estimates agree within 10%, so we use $2.43 billion.

## Serviceable market

Not every mid-market company is a buyer. We count only companies that meet two
conditions. First, they run one of the three ERPs we integrate with natively,
which together hold about 60% of the mid-market. Second, they still match
invoices by hand or on a legacy capture tool, which surveys of our pipeline and
Halvorsen's buyer panel put at a little under two thirds of that group. Together
those filters leave about 35% of the band, or 22,400 companies, and a
serviceable market of $851 million.

## Why now

In June 2025, Corvane, the most widely used mid-market ERP, announced that it
will retire its bundled AP module at the end of 2026. Corvane has told customers
to move to a partner product and has not named a preferred one. That decision
forces roughly 9,000 finance teams to choose a new AP tool on a fixed deadline.
Tallyroom was certified as a Corvane integration partner in August 2025. Half
of our current pipeline comes from Corvane customers.

## Revenue plan

Our plan is based on market share. Capturing 1% of an $851 million serviceable
market is $8.5 million in ARR, and 1% is a conservative figure for a product
that already outperforms the legacy tools on cost per invoice. We plan to
capture 1% in year one and add a point of share each year after that, reaching
5% of the serviceable market, or $42.6 million in ARR, by the end of year five.

We think this trajectory is realistic because the market is fragmented. No
single AP vendor holds more than 12% of the mid-market, and most companies in
our serviceable market are not replacing a competitor at all; they are replacing
a spreadsheet and a shared inbox.

## Comparable companies

The mid-market AP category has produced a range of outcomes. Paylode reached
$60 million in ARR in seven years by selling through ERP resellers. Invoxa
reached $18 million and then stalled when its main ERP partner launched a
competing module, and was sold in 2024 at roughly two times revenue. Capturely
raised $40 million, never passed $6 million in ARR, and shut down in 2023; its
former executives describe a product that worked only for companies with
standardised purchase orders. The lesson we take is that ERP dependence cuts
both ways, which is why we integrate with three ERPs and not one.

## Use of funds

The $12M round funds product work on multi-entity support, a second
engineering team for the ERP integrations, and marketing. We expect the round to
last 30 months.
