# E1 results: is full-pass cost findings-dependent? (2026-08-06)

**Design**: `review-canon.md` §3 E1. Full pipeline pass-1 (k=3 fact-check merged
most-severe-wins + opus critics + rubric synthesis, no fix loop) on the dirty and
post-fix states of 3 canon instances, in commit-pinned detached worktrees
(contamination guard). All artifacts + per-stage token accounting in
`runs/review-arms/e1/` (`manifest.json` is the ledger).
**Spend**: **5,259,083 subagent tokens** across 6 cells / 54 agents (+ orchestration).

## The answer: pass cost is findings-independent. Fixed-pass model confirmed 3/3 pairs.

| Pair | Dirty tokens (verdict) | Clean tokens (verdict) | clean/dirty |
|---|---|---|---|
| csp (72→142 line diffs) | 817,597 (4R/12A) | 898,006 (4R/10A) | **1.098** |
| fscompat (21→43) | 735,602 (4R/15A) | 729,684 (1R/12A) | **0.992** |
| corpus (1166→~1210, app/ scope) | 1,061,646 (4R/17A) | 1,016,548 (5R/15A) | **0.958** |

Mean ratio ≈ **1.02**. Cost variation tracks diff size (csp-clean is the pair's larger
diff and the only clean>dirty cost), never finding count — a 4R→1R drop (fscompat)
bought a 0.8% cost change. Per-stage the pattern is uniform: every clean critic ran
within ±15% of its dirty twin. Scaling with diff size is strongly **sublinear**: the
corpus diff is ~15× csp's and cost only ~1.3× — reading is targeted, not exhaustive.

**Consequence for P-B (lite-first)**: a lite pre-loop saves *zero* pass-1 cost on the
subsequent full review. Its entire value is **iterations avoided** (E2's answer: ~1
iteration on the ~40% of instances whose defects are in-diff behavioral). The E1+E2
composite estimate for P-B: expected saving ≈ 0.4 × pass-cost ≈ ~300k tokens/instance,
for ~$0.10 of lite spend — positive but modest, and stratum-dependent.

## The second, larger result: "clean" states are never clean — and fixes breed defects

All three post-fix states failed review (4R, 1R, 5R). Two mechanisms, both observed
directly:

1. **Fix commits introduce defects.** csp's fix added the vacuous test-regex guard
   (`/\bhttp:\b/` can never match; 3/3 replicates proved it by execution). corpus's fix
   added the C2 production hard-refuse whose `typeof process !== "undefined"` gating is
   asymmetric — the guard is skipped exactly when the localStorage enable path (gated by
   nothing) still fires: **the hardening fails open**. The fix pass is itself a defect
   source, and the historical loops never re-reviewed their own fixes with fresh eyes.
2. **Fresh passes find different layers.** corpus-clean's R1 (rehydration bypasses the
   seam; `migrateFromV2()` re-fires over corpus state every load) existed in the dirty
   state too — the dirty pass didn't surface it. Review verdicts are draws from a
   distribution (the 0.14–0.25 red-band self-agreement result, again); a loop that
   terminates on "re-review is clean" is actually terminating on reviewer-novelty
   exhaustion, not defect exhaustion. The historical ≤3-iteration cap + human punt is
   the right containment for this, and "full review on a clean commit" is not only just
   as expensive — it is nearly as *loud*.

## Composition verdict (P-A vs P-B vs P-C)

- **P-A (full loop only)**: each iteration costs a full pass (~730k-1.06M tokens
  observed, sublinear in diff size). Nothing about a cleaner input makes iterations
  cheaper.
- **P-B (lite-first)**: keep — it is nearly free ($0.08-0.25) and clears the red on the
  in-diff-behavioral stratum before the expensive loop starts — but expect one saved
  iteration on a minority of instances, not a multiplier.
- **P-C (staged interleaving)**: E1 strengthens its premise more than expected, for a
  reason beyond cost: **fixes applied between stages get reviewed by the remaining
  stages of the same pass.** Both observed fix-introduced defects (vacuous regexes,
  fails-open guard) were exactly the kind of thing the *same pass's* later critics
  catch (the clean cells' fact-check/security did catch them — one pass later, at full
  price). P-C converts "fix defects found by the next $10 pass" into "fix defects found
  by the remaining stages of the current pass". Design E3 around that property; the
  churn risk (030) still applies between iterations, not within a pass.

## Side observations worth keeping

- **Replicate agreement tracks state cleanliness** (csp: 53% dirty → 89% clean;
  fscompat: 85%→86%; corpus: 77%→82%). Verdict-stability might be usable as a cheap
  cleanliness signal — free with every k=3 run.
- **The 2026-08 panel is much harsher than the 2026-04 loops** (historical: 2R/4A,
  1R/4A, 0R/4A on these same ranges vs 4R/12A, 4R/15A, 4R/17A now). Partly better
  briefs/skills (029-validated), partly opus panel, partly measurement-mode
  all-severities. Canon labels from historical rubrics are a *floor*, not the ceiling.
- **New later-fix-checkable label candidates** (append-only, pending adjudication at
  HEAD): the vacuous test regexes (csp-clean R1 — check if ever fixed); the fails-open
  C2 guard (corpus-clean R2); the rehydration bypass (corpus-clean R1); the
  "fresh ArrayBuffer view" comment (3/3, both corpus states); the OPFS write race not
  on the author's carry list; cacheKey-in-cache-file + double-hash (fscompat, both
  states). Each needs one grep at current HEAD to classify fixed/live.
- The historical fix commits' claimed review outcomes ("0 red, 4 amber") and the E1
  rerun's verdicts on the same code differ by an entire tier — worth remembering when
  reading any rubric as ground truth: labels are pipeline-relative.
