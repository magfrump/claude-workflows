# Stockvet: Seed Memo

## Summary

Stockvet is inventory and reordering software for independent veterinary
clinics. A clinic plugs Stockvet into its practice-management system, and we
track what is dispensed, predict when each product will run out, and place
reorders with the clinic's distributor automatically. We are raising a $3.5M
seed round to take the product from 41 paying clinics in Oregon and Washington
to a regional footprint across the western United States.

## The problem

A typical independent clinic stocks between 400 and 1,200 SKUs: vaccines,
parasiticides, prescription diets, surgical consumables and controlled
substances. Most clinics manage this with a spreadsheet and a practice manager
who walks the shelves on Friday afternoons. The results are predictable. In our
pilot clinics, the practice manager spent a median of 6.5 hours a week on
ordering, and each clinic reported at least two stock-outs of a revenue item in
the prior quarter. Expired stock written off at year end ran between 2% and 4%
of inventory value.

## Market size

The global animal health market was worth $62 billion in 2025, according to
Meridian Research Partners, and it is growing at 8% a year. Veterinary clinics
are the point of sale for the large majority of that spend: almost every
product in the category passes through a clinic's shelves or its pharmacy
before it reaches an animal. Stockvet sits at exactly that point, on the
clinic's shelf, which is why we treat the full $62 billion as our total
addressable market. Every dollar in the category is a dollar that some clinic
has to forecast, order, store and track.

Our serviceable market is narrower. There are roughly 32,000 veterinary clinics
in the United States, of which about 18,000 are independently owned; the rest
belong to corporate groups that run centralised procurement and are not our
buyer. Of the 18,000 independents, we count only clinics that already run one
of the four practice-management systems we integrate with and that stock more
than 400 SKUs, because below that size the ordering pain is not worth a
subscription. That leaves about 11,000 clinics. At our current price of $400 a
month ($4,800 a year), the serviceable market is $52.8 million in annual
recurring revenue.

## Go-to-market and share

We will not sell nationally at first. Our beachhead is the 1,200 qualifying
clinics in the Pacific Northwest and Mountain states, where our founding team
has worked as practice managers and relief veterinarians, and where the two
regional veterinary associations have agreed to feature Stockvet at their
annual meetings. Our target is 25% of that beachhead (300 clinics) by the end of
year three, sold by three field reps who each close roughly eight clinics a
month at full ramp. We then expand into California and Texas. By the end of
year five we project 700 clinics, or about 6% of the serviceable market, and
$3.4 million in ARR.

## Why now

Until last year, reordering from a veterinary distributor meant logging into
that distributor's own portal. In March 2025, both of the large national
distributors, Pallas Animal Supply and Northgate Veterinary Distribution, opened
electronic ordering APIs to third-party software for the first time. Those two
distributors handle most of the product an independent clinic buys. Before the
APIs existed, automated reordering was not possible without screen-scraping,
which both distributors prohibited. Stockvet was built in the six months after
the APIs launched.

## Comparable companies

The closest analogues sell inventory software to other independent clinical
practices. Dentrack, which serves independent dental offices, reached 2,100
practices six years after launch. Optistock tried the same model in optometry,
stalled at about 400 practices, and was acquired for its team in 2023; its
founders have told us that optometry practices carry too few SKUs for the
savings to justify the price. Clinicshelf, a general-purpose medical inventory
tool, never found a segment and closed in 2022. We think veterinary clinics
look more like dental offices than optometry: high SKU counts, perishable
stock, and a single owner-operator who feels the cost of stock-outs directly.

## The raise

The $3.5M seed funds the three field reps, two engineers for the California and
Texas practice-management integrations, and 24 months of runway. At the end of
that period we expect to be at 300 clinics and ready to raise a Series A on the
strength of the beachhead.
