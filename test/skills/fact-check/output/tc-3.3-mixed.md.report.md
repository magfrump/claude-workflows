## Verdict for C1: "The childcare sector employs roughly 1 million workers in the United States."

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

Several BLS measures land near 1 million. Federal payroll data (BLS Current Employment Statistics) counted about 1 million workers in the child care industry at the start of 2020. A BLS Monthly Labor Review article using the Quarterly Census of Employment and Wages (QCEW) counts over 942,000 workers at childcare establishments. The BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook counts about 991,600 childcare-worker jobs in 2024. There is a scope caveat: the narrower May 2022 occupational wage survey for "childcare workers" (the source of C2's figure) counts only about 459,000, because it excludes self-employed and household workers. So "1 million" holds for the sector, not for the occupation group that the $13.71 figure describes. The two BLS sources are independent primary sources, but I only read excerpts, so confidence is capped at Medium.

**Sources:** BLS Occupational Outlook Handbook, Childcare Workers (2024 data); BLS Monthly Labor Review (QCEW); BLS OEWS May 2022 (39-9011)
**Citation:** "Childcare workers held about 991,600 jobs in 2024." — [BLS OOH](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/personal-care-and-service/childcare-workers.htm#tab-3)

## Verdict for C2: "The median pay for childcare workers was $13.71 per hour in 2022"

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The BLS occupational wage survey for May 2022 (childcare workers, occupation code 39-9011) lists a median hourly wage of $13.71, with a mean of $14.22. A National Women's Law Center (NWLC) fact sheet reports the same figure. The excerpt states the exact number, so it counts as a primary source, but a full read of the BLS table plus a second independent primary source would be needed for High. For context, the median rose to $14.60 in May 2023 and $15.41 in May 2024.

**Sources:** BLS OEWS May 2022, 39-9011 (primary); NWLC, "Child Care Dollars" fact sheet, 2023 (secondary)
**Citation:** [BLS OEWS May 2022, Childcare Workers](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2022/may/oes399011.htm#:~:text=13.71)

## Verdict for C3: "Many states have waiting lists for subsidized care slots"

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

NWLC's annual surveys of state childcare assistance policies count states with waiting lists or frozen intake: 19 in 2018, 15 in 2019, 13 in February 2024 and 17 in February 2025. NPR's 2026 reporting also cites 17 states. I couldn't retrieve the 2023 count, but in every year found, a substantial minority of states had waiting lists.

**Sources:** NWLC press release (2025); NWLC State Child Care Assistance Policies 2018/2019; NPR (2026)
**Citation:** "17 states had waiting lists or a freeze on intake for child care assistance in February 2025, up from 13 states in February 2024" — [NWLC](https://nwlc.org/press-release/nwlc-finds-waiting-lists-for-child-care-assistance-nearly-doubled-in-2025/)

## Verdict for C4: "in Florida, the waitlist exceeded 40,000 families in 2023"

**Verdict:** Unverified
**Confidence:** Low
**Provenance:** assumed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

I found no statewide count of Florida's School Readiness waitlist for 2023. The figures I did find don't support "over 40,000 families":
- About 15,230 children in 2019 and about 4,200 in 2022, per Florida Department of Education data cited in news reports.
- "Just over 14,900" for the whole state in January 2025, per Children's Movement Florida.

The same data-hub page shows a "School Readiness Program Waitlist" value of 45,050, but its date and scope couldn't be confirmed. Florida also counts *children*, not *families*, which is a unit mismatch with the draft. Moving this claim to observed would take Florida Division of Early Learning monthly waitlist data for 2023, or a dated source for the 45,050 figure.

**Sources:** Children's Movement Florida Early Childhood Data Hub (undated page); news report citing Florida DOE (via Yahoo News); Florida DEL 2023–24 Annual Report (not retrievable)
**Citation:** "In January 2025, the number was just over 14,900 for the entire state." — [Children's Movement Florida Data Hub](https://www.childrensmovementflorida.org/data-hub)

## Verdict for C5: "Denmark spends approximately 2% of GDP on early childhood education and care"

**Verdict:** Inaccurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

OECD data puts Denmark's early childhood education and care (ECEC) spending at about 1.2–1.3% of GDP, not 2%. The OECD country profile reports total ECEC spending of 1.3% of GDP in 2014, against an OECD average of 0.8%. The OECD Family Database says public spending is "above 1.0%" in the Nordic countries. People's Policy Project gives 1.2–1.3%. No source I found supports about 2%. Because this verdict asks for a correction, it should rest on a full read, but the OECD PDF couldn't be opened. Confidence therefore stays at Medium until the PF3.1 spreadsheet is checked directly.

**Sources:** OECD, "Providing Quality Early Childhood Education and Care," Denmark profile (2014 data); OECD Family Database PF3.1 (2019/latest); People's Policy Project, "Childcare in the US and Nordic Countries" (secondary)
**Citation:** [OECD Denmark ECEC country profile](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/providing-quality-early-childhood-education-and-care_301005d1-en/full-report/component-13.html), which reports ECEC expenditure of 1.3% of GDP (2014)

## Verdict for C6: "compared to about 0.4% in the US"

**Verdict:** Mostly accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** inferred
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The OECD Family Database places the US "below 0.4%" of GDP for public ECEC spending. People's Policy Project gives 0.27–0.3%, and an older OECD paper gives 0.1–0.3%. Putting these together, the US figure is about 0.3%, so "about 0.4%" is a ceiling rather than a point estimate and slightly overstates US spending.

**Sources:** OECD Family Database PF3.1; People's Policy Project; OECD, "Is Childcare Affordable?"
**Citation:** "below 0.4% in the United States" — [OECD PF3.1](https://webfs.oecd.org/els-com/Family_Database/PF3_1_Public_spending_on_childcare_and_early_education.pdf)
