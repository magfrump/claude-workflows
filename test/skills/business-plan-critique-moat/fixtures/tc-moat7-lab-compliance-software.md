# Assayra — One-Page Strategy

Assayra is laboratory information software for accredited environmental
testing labs: the 40-to-200-person labs that test drinking water, soil and
wastewater for utilities and regulators. We run 212 labs at an average contract
of $48,000 a year, on three-year terms.

**Where the advantage comes from.** An accredited lab cannot change its sample
tracking system casually. Every test method a lab runs is validated inside the
system that records it, and its accreditation body audits that record. Moving to
a new system means re-validating each method and passing a follow-up audit, which
our customers put at four to nine months of a quality manager's time. We have
lost 3 labs in four years, two of them to acquisitions. The advantage is switching cost backed by regulation. We are not
yet large enough to claim scale economies, and we do not claim a network effect:
one lab's use of Assayra does nothing for another's.

**How we sell.** New labs are won by six field account executives
over a four-to-seven-month cycle. A $48,000 annual contract on a three-year term supports that motion, and a cheaper channel would
not reach the lab director and quality manager who sign together. About a third of
pipeline comes from referrals at the two national lab associations' meetings,
where our customers present their audit results; the rest is outbound. No single
channel supplies more than half of new contracts, and no platform sits between us
and the buyer.

**The hard part is new labs, not existing ones.** The same re-validation cost
that keeps our customers makes a lab running a competitor's system slow to
switch to us. Our growth therefore depends on catching labs at natural moments:
a new accreditation, a merger, or an end-of-life notice from their current
vendor.

**What incumbents will do.** Two horizontal lab-software vendors serve pharma and
clinical labs and could build an environmental module. If they do, we expect
them to bundle it cheaply into their existing suites to win the handful of
environmental labs owned by their larger customers, and to undercut us on
price for greenfield labs. We cannot stop that. What it does not change is our
installed base, which would face the same re-validation cost to move to them.
We plan for our share of new labs to fall if a vendor enters, and our three-year
plan holds up with half our current win rate on new labs.

**What we are asking for.** $9M to add four account executives, build connectors
to the three state reporting portals our customers file with most, and reach
400 labs in three years.
