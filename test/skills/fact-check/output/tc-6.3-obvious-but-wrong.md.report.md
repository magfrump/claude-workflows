## Verdict for C1: "The Great Wall of China is … visible from space"

**Verdict:** Disputed
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** inferred
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The most natural reading is "visible to the naked eye from orbit." NASA's feature says the wall generally can't be seen with the unaided eye from low Earth orbit, and certainly not from the Moon. China's first astronaut, Yang Liwei, said he couldn't pick it out during his 2003 mission. The well-known 2004 Expedition 10 photo needed a telephoto lens. On the other side, astronauts Eugene Cernan and Ed Lu have said it can be seen from the lower part of low Earth orbit, but only in very good conditions. Britannica says it is "only sometimes" visible from low orbit. Under a looser reading (seen with cameras or radar from orbit), the claim holds. This verdict is capped at Medium because no full source could be opened. Reading NASA's feature and the Wikipedia article's sources in full would firm up the astronaut accounts.

**Sources:** NASA, "China's Wall Less Great in View from Space" (2005); Wikipedia, "Artificial structures visible from space"; Britannica Q&A; Scientific American; Space.com
**Citation:** https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/static/history/PDFs/great_wall_less_great_from_space.pdf
**Sources disagree:** NASA's 2005 feature and Yang Liwei say the wall generally isn't visible to the naked eye from low orbit, while astronauts Eugene Cernan and Ed Lu (as cited by Wikipedia) say it is visible from lower low Earth orbit under very favorable conditions.

## Verdict for C2: "the only man-made structure visible from space"

**Verdict:** Inaccurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** observed
**Scrutiny:** abstract

Many man-made features can be seen from orbit, and several are easier to see than the Great Wall. Examples include cities at night, highways, dams, the Bingham Canyon Mine, the Palm Jumeirah and the Suez Canal. NASA's own feature describes the "only man-made object visible from space" idea as a common claim that it says is generally untrue. The myth goes back at least to a 1932 Ripley's cartoon, which said the wall would be visible from the Moon. This verdict is capped at Medium because the NASA primary source was seen only through a search summary. A full read of it, plus a second primary source such as astronaut imagery records, would lift this to High.

**Sources:** NASA (2005); Wikipedia, "Artificial structures visible from space"; Space.com, "What human-made structures can be seen from space?"; Ripley's; Snopes
**Citation:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_structures_visible_from_space

## Verdict for C3: "employed millions of workers"

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** inferred
**Scrutiny:** abstract

Several secondary sources agree that millions of people worked on the wall across its history. No exact count exists, so "millions" is an estimate. Records cited for the Northern Qi dynasty alone put the number at about 1.8 million. Figures for the Qin phase are in the hundreds of thousands. The workforce was largely soldiers, drafted peasants and convicts, not hired labor. "Employed" holds if it means "used." This rests on matching secondary sources, which is Medium. A scholarly source on dynastic labor records would strengthen it.

**Sources:** History.com (Nate Barksdale); TravelChinaGuide, "Who built the Great Wall"; China Highlights; History Collection
**Citation:** https://www.history.com/articles/great-wall-china-builders

## Verdict for C4: "over centuries"

**Verdict:** Accurate
**Confidence:** Medium
**Provenance:** inferred
**Scrutiny:** abstract

The wall was built in stages from about the 7th century BC, starting with walls built by separate states during the Spring and Autumn period. Qin Shi Huang linked those walls together, later dynasties extended them, and much of what stands today was built by the Ming dynasty (1368–1644). That is roughly two thousand years, so "over centuries" is correct, if anything an understatement. Sources disagree on the exact start date, end date and number of dynasties, but none of that changes this verdict. Capped at Medium because only search summaries were read.

**Sources:** Britannica, "Great Wall of China"; Wikipedia, "History of the Great Wall of China"; TravelChinaGuide chronology; China Highlights
**Citation:** https://www.britannica.com/topic/Great-Wall-of-China/The-Han-through-Yuan-dynasties
